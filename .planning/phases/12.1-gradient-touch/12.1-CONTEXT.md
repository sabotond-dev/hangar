## D-01 [user, 2026-09-11] The finding and the shape

Verbatim, from the bench note:

> calibration is still waay off. i need to tap multiple times on a LED to react. putting a fingertip
> between two leds and nothing happens, its just not reliable at all.

> not really. for example if my finger is directly on top of the LED in row 2 column 8 the top right
> LED lights up, same in each corne. so its not precise.
> I mean it never can be precise as the motion detector are not directly under the LEDs. that's why
> each config should work with gradient lighting. so if i touch between two LEDs both should light up
> dimly and if I move in one direction that should be brighter. the same should happen if I touch in
> the middle of a 4 LEDs. each should light up and only the brightness should change depending how I
> move my fingers. The module doesnt only send 9x9 messages, it sends a lot more. LEDs should
> indicate that

**This is the specification.** A finger is drawn bilinearly across the 2x2 LEDs around it, weighted
by distance; between two LEDs both glow; in the middle of four all four glow; the brightness follows
the finger continuously. Any discrete choice an entry needs (EUCLID's step, STEPS' cell, CONSOLE's
column) is taken from the same calibrated coordinate the light is drawn from, so the toggle and the
glow agree on where the LEDs are. The orchestrator's reading, confirmed by the user ("continue"):
two problems in order - (1) the sensor-to-LED map is uncalibrated, (2) Phase 12's library renders a
finger as one cell. Both are this phase's.

## D-02 [user, via the orchestrator] Calibration is measured, not assumed - and the planner's check of the derivation

The knot tables come from Probe C, run 1 (11:43:50 - 11:44:31, 2026-09-11), and from nowhere else.
One TypeScript file, `src/lib/catalog/calibration.ts`, holds them and derives the Lua tables, the
simulator's forward map and every test expectation (research B.5). No number is typed twice.

**The derivation, checked by the planner.** Targets 1..9 are cells 36..44 (row 4) and give `KX`
directly; targets 11..17 are cells 13, 22, 31, 49, 58, 67, 76 (column 4 without the centre) and target
5 (cell 40, `y = 68`) supplies `KY[4]`; target 10 was to be cell 4 (row 0). Its reading `(127, 68)` is
`x` pinned at the right edge with `y` on row 4 - a re-tap after a dropped lift at target 9, not a
row-0 reading - and **discarding it is right**. `KY[0]` is then taken from corners 18 `(1, 0)` and 19
`(127, 0)`, both `y = 0`. The separability check passes: 18 reads `(1, 0)` against `(KX[0], KY[0]) =
(1, 0)`; 19 `(127, 0)` against `(126, 0)`; 20 `(0, 127)` against `(1, 126)`; 21 `(127, 126)` against
`(126, 126)` - every corner within 1 of the product of the two axis tables, under the research's
3-unit rule. The tables are strictly increasing on both axes, so `U`'s floor-division by a knot
difference never sees zero.

```
KX = 1, 13, 29, 46, 64, 85, 100, 120, 126   steps 12, 16, 17, 18, 21, 15, 20, 6
KY = 0, 12, 26, 48, 68, 83,  97, 115, 126   steps  -, 14, 22, 20, 15, 14, 18, 11
```

**Agreed, with one caveat the orchestrator did not draw.** A corner finger saturates both axes, so
corners 18 and 19 can only say `KY[0] <= about 2`; they cannot distinguish `0` from a small positive
value the way target 10 would have (the low side of `x` compresses to a 12-unit step, so a centred
row-0 finger may plausibly read a few units above 0). The only thing that value moves is the blend
between row 0 and row 1 - at worst a third of that one pitch, at the top row only. **So: the tables
are frozen from run 1 now (`MEASURED = true`, provenance `runs: 1`, target 10 named as discarded), a
second pass is NOT needed before freezing, and a five-minute direct reading of target 10 (plus an
optional whole second pass) is the phase's first calibration bench row.** If it moves any knot by more
than 2 units, `calibration.ts`'s two arrays are edited and `frames.json` regenerated - one commit, no
plan re-opened.

The saturation the research inferred is confirmed and sharper than inferred: the high side of both
axes runs out of range inside the outer LED (LED 7 to LED 8 is 6 units in `x`, 11 in `y`), the low
side compresses less (12 and 14), and the middle is ~17 per LED. The user's "row 2 / column 8 lights
the corner" follows exactly: `x = 120` under `x*9//128` is column 8.

## D-03 [user, 2026-09-11] 255/6 as the library's second slot - answered "yes"

The research's only fit: the gradient beside Q6.5's expiry machinery does not fit one slot in any
variant (the smallest, 1,043, is 135 over; every variant is on record in `12.1-RESEARCH.md` B.3 and
re-measured on the real knots by the planner). The system element's Timer event (255/6) is a
908-character slot nobody has claimed (`SLOT-ARITHMETIC.md` §2: *"nothing, never asked"*), D-19
retired the refusal to write it, and the cross-slot call is proved on the user's ZONA twice. Split:
**255/0 at 782 (126 free), 255/6 at 683 (225 free)**, joined by `self:tim()`. Both figures reproduced
by the planner on the measured knots, canonical, `checkSyntax` true.

**Asked and answered.** Asked *"Use 255/6 (the system Timer) as the library's second slot? It means
HANGAR writes a fourth string to the module and PUT BACK restores it"*, the user answered: **"yes"**
(2026-09-11, recorded in `BENCH-2026-09-11.txt` under "Decision, 2026-09-11"). Phase 12.1 builds the
library across 255/0 and 255/6. **Plan 01 records the answer with the figures re-run beside it and
asks nothing** - the way 13-02 recorded D-18 - so the three branches that were on the table are on
record with their measured costs:

| Branch | What it is | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| **`two-slots`** | the research's fit; a fourth string on the wire, a fourth snapshot field, every count of three becoming four, 13-17's 255/4 the fifth row of the slot list | 255/0 **782**, 255/6 **683** |
| **`calibration-only`** | one slot, no gradient: `Q` reads the measured map through `U`, so the outer-ring taps and the row 2 / column 8 corner are fixed, but **the finger stays one cell and the user's specification is not met**; `D` is dropped (TRACKPAD inlines its decay idiom, +26 in a Timer with 398 free), `N` is not shipped (ARC keeps its naive centre test) | **882**, 26 free |
| **`stop`** | the phase stops; Phase 12's library stands at 769 with the naive divisor | - |

**The orchestrator's phrase "a reduced gradient with no expiry clear" is not a branch that fits.** That
is the research's variant H - linear `U`, `N`, `D`, `A` dropped and the expiry block clear removed -
and it measures 1,043, still 135 over. A "reduced gradient" would have to drop the hysteresis or the
expiry, which the probe's six rules forbid. The honest one-slot branch is calibration without the
gradient, and plan 01's record says so in those words.

The answer is `two-slots`. D-19 permits the write; PUT BACK restores it; CLEAR writes the firmware's own 255/6
default; the snapshot goes to its next version with the fourth string; a record from before this phase
restores the 255/6 default, as v1 records restored the 255/0 default at 12-03.

## D-04 [user asked, NOT yet answered] KEEP once during the bench, for the page-load proof

The one thing two probes did not tap: the system `ini` calling the system `tim` on a **page load**
(research B.6). At install time the 255/6 write runs its body and 255/0's `self:tim()` merely re-runs

library to the user's flash once (PUT BACK restores it; CLEAR + KEEP leaves nothing behind). **Carried
as a runbook row, not a checkpoint**: the user answers it by doing it, or by declining, and the gate

the fallback is to carry 255/6's functions in 255/4 (13-17's slot, same mechanism, same doubt) or to
ask the firmware side - the gate records the outcome; nothing in this phase assumes it.

## D-11 [planner, from the research] The primitive's shape

As researched and re-run on the measured knots, and not otherwise:

- **255/0 (system Setup, written second):** `--[[@cb]]H={}T={}C=0 P={}B={}L=0 KX={..}KY={..}` then
  `U W E Q X` and `self:tim()`. State and the map. `U(v,k)` is the calibrated axis in 64ths of a pitch
  (LED `n` at `n*64`, 0..512); `W(u,p)` holds the previous LED while the calibrated coordinate is
  within 45/64 of its centre; `E` also clears the contact's block through `V`; `Q`'s contract is
  unchanged for its eight callers; `X` unchanged.
- **255/6 (system Timer, written FIRST):** `--[[@cb]]` then `V G N A D`. Painters and senders.
  `G(s,i,e,x,y,l,b)` is the bilinear finger on layer `l` at peak phase `b`, integer weights
  `(64-f)(64-g)`, `f(64-g)`, `(64-f)g`, `fg` over 4096; `V(n)` clears a block; `N(x,y)` is the nearest
  calibrated cell with no hysteresis and no state; `A` and `D` move here unchanged.
- **The hysteresis is not retired** (research finding 3): the gradient removes the cell for the light,
  not for the toggle; a bare argmax would put Q2's flicker back into the note.
- **`N` ships with a caller today** - ARC's stop tap `N(x,y)==40` - and a named future one (13-15's
  region lookup). 12-07's rule stands: a function with no caller is not shipped. `F` stays dropped.
- **Layer 0 everywhere** for the finger, including RADAR POINTS and SONAR, whose centre marker is
  re-lit after every `G` call (D-15).
- **Write order 255/6, 255/0, 0/6, 0/0**, on the same rule that already puts 0/6 before 0/0: a written
  body runs at once, and 255/0's `self:tim()` needs the method registered first.
- **`LIBRARY_VERSION` stays "1"** unless the executor finds a dropped name: the rule in `library.ts`
  bumps it for a removal, not for an addition or a change, and this revision removes nothing from
  `_G` on a live module. The executor states which it is.

## D-12 [planner, research Q2] Linear weights

Shipped linear. The firmware's phase-to-output curve is already convex (research B.1: half weight
lands on 25 % duty, which the eye reads as roughly 55-60 %), so linear weights read close to linear.
The square-law `G` is costed at +15 (255/6 at 698) and is not the default. **A bench row asks whether
the dim end reads "dimly"** - the user answers by looking, and a "too bright" answer is a fifteen-

## D-13 [planner, research Q3] The finger colour is a literal per entry, not a knob

White for EUCLID, STEPS, CHORUS, CONSOLE; the entry's own colour where one exists - `@SWEEPC` for
RADAR POINTS and SONAR, `@TRAILC` for MORPH, the cell's palette colour for LUMEN. A knob would move
every entry's shape character and demote every captured stamp (the POMODORO precedent) - CONT-03's
"no change to any knob's value count without stating the shape-character consequence" is the reason
this did not need asking. **A bench row asks the one thing the planner cannot see: whether one colour
everywhere would read better.** Changing a literal later costs nothing in shape.

## D-14 [planner, research Q4] `A`'s CC pair stays raw

`A` sends raw `x` and `127-y`, unchanged and moved to 255/6. It is the user's own bench snippet
(12-07), LUMEN is its one caller, and calibrating it (`U(x,KX)*127//512`, about +25) would change what
a DAW receives from the only entry that sends a position - a behaviour change to an instrument, made
silently. **Not this phase's to make; a bench row asks whether LUMEN's CC reaches 0 and 127 where the
user expects it to**, with the consequence written beside it (raw reaches 0/127 a third of an LED
inside the outer LEDs).

## D-18 [planner, research Q11] The hold band stays at 45/64 of a pitch - and it is a fraction of the pitch, not a width

Kept. On the measured knots the band between LED 4 and LED 5 in `x` (pitch 21) is nine values wide
(70..78, switch-up at 79, switch-down at 69), not the research's seven; on the outer `x` segment

width 12-07 measured"**, which is true only where the pitch happens to be 17. The smoke test asserts
the band as a function of the table, never as a literal. A bench row asks whether a finger resting on
the seam between two ring cells ever toggles both.
