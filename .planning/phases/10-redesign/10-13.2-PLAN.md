## Defect 1 - the fix that was chosen, and the one that was not

The brief offered two shapes and asked which, and why.

> **(a) Knock the field back** so it can never compete with a glyph. One declaration - an `opacity` on
> the pseudo-element, keeping the token and the 2:1 ratio between the field and the distinguished
> cross intact. Cheapest thing that could work.
>
> **(b) Stop the lattice painting under the surfaces that carry prose**, so it lives in the margins,
> the gaps and around the pads - which is what the reference actually does.

**(b) is chosen, and it is reachable in FOUR declarations across four files with no new node on any
panel.** The reason (a) was rejected is not that it fails to help - it does help - but that it
**changes the symptom's amplitude and not its cause**. A cross would still sit on the `H` of `HANGAR`
and on the `y` of *Everything on the shelf*; it would just be a fainter cross. The user's brief says
the marks belong *in the surround, not across the subject*, and that is a statement about **position**.
An opacity is not a position.

### The rule, stated once

> **The lattice is a GROUND, and a ground is visible exactly where nothing is standing on it.**

The mechanism is paint order, and it is the same mechanism 10-13.1 had to learn the hard way. The
lattice is a `z-index: -1` pseudo-element inside the root's own stacking context, so it paints after
the root's background and before the root's content. **Anything in the content that declares an
opaque background occludes it.** So:

```css
.lattice > :where(*) {
  background-color: var(--color-ground);
}
```

**`:where()` is load-bearing and is not a stylistic choice.** It zeroes the compound's specificity, so
the whole selector weighs `0,1,0` - the same as a bare class. Consequences, each of which is the
reason:

1. **A Svelte-scoped component rule beats it.** Scoped styles compile to `.foo.svelte-<hash>` at
   `0,2,0`, so any component that has an opinion about its own root background keeps it, automatically
   and without being listed anywhere. The rule is a **default**, not an override.
2. **`.pill`'s `background: transparent` beats it**, because `.pill` is `0,1,0` and is declared later
   in the same file. A control that happens to be a direct child of a lattice root therefore keeps its
   declared fill rather than silently gaining an opaque box.
3. **It costs no node.** The brief's condition for preferring (b) was *"if you can reach it without
   adding a node to every panel"*. This adds none - not a wrapper, not a pseudo-element, not a class
   attribute on a single component.

### The three declared exceptions, each with its reason

| File | Selector | Declaration | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `src/routes/browse/+page.svelte` | `.grid` | `background-color: transparent` | **The one block that must NOT occlude.** The card wall is where the lattice earns its keep: it paints in the gutters between cards, which is the "around the pads" half of the brief. Occluding the grid would delete the field from 80% of the page |
| `src/lib/ui/CatalogCard.svelte` | `.card` | `background-color: var(--color-ground)` | The card is not a direct child of a lattice root, so the ground rule cannot reach it. It takes the ground itself, which is what turns the grid's exception from "the lattice paints over thirty-six descriptions" into "the lattice paints between thirty-six cards" |
| `src/lib/ui/BrowseGrid.svelte` | `.empty` | `background-color: var(--color-ground)` | The empty state renders **instead of** the card wall, inside the same transparent grid, and it is three lines of prose. Without this the one screen where the page has nothing to show is the one screen where the lattice is worst. Found by the DOM walk in task 1, not by reading the template |

**There is no fourth exception, and scan 6 asserts that by walking rather than by trusting.**

### What this costs Layer G, stated rather than discovered

The ground occluders also hide **Layer G's 3px halftone** wherever they paint, because an opaque
background occludes everything behind it and Layer G is `body::before` at `z-index: -1`, behind
everything.

**Layer G's declaration does not change, its route scope does not change, and `aesthetic.e2e.ts`
test 3 - which asserts the browse grid carries Layer G and nothing else - is untouched.** What changes
is how much **bare ground** the page has for it to show through. The cost is computable and is
computed rather than estimated: the halftone dot is `rgb(214 255 78 / 0.04)` in a 3px cell with a
solid core out to 40% of the cell, so its area-averaged lift over black is
`0.04 x pi x 0.4^2 ~= 0.008` of the token, i.e. **about 1 part in 255 on the green channel** - below
the threshold at which a difference is visible on an unlit display, and dithered at 3px so it reads as
grain rather than as a cast. The vignette half of Layer G is `rgb(0 0 0 / 0.55)` over
`--color-ground: #000000`, which is black over black and costs literally nothing.

**Record the number, do not hide the change.** This is A-57.

## Defect 2 - the fix that was chosen, and what it may not do

**What is forbidden, and none of it is done:** no invented lit cells, no animation, no change to what
the configuration does, no gesture authored to light it, no demo path, no entry-specific branch.
`restsBlack: true`, `DARK_BY_CONSTRUCTION`, `demoPathFor("tpad") === undefined`,
`front-door.ts`'s `EXCLUDED_FROM_ROW` entry and `frames.spec.ts`'s both-directions walk are all
**byte-untouched**, and `browse.e2e.ts`'s single exemption still names Trackpad.

**Why the card looks broken, diagnosed rather than guessed.** `PadFrame.svelte` builds a pad face from
four layers, and the one that draws the cell structure is **Layer 3, the gutter grid**, which is
painted in `var(--color-ground)` on top of the canvas. It is black. It is legible on every other card
only because the LIT cells behind it are not. On an all-unlit face there is nothing for a black line
to divide, so 81 lamps collapse into one rectangle. Layer 1's dot field survives - the dots are
visible on Trackpad today - but nine rows of nine dots on black is a dot grid, not a pad.

**The fix: the unlit cell is drawn as a CELL.** `.dots` gains one declaration:

```css
background-color: color-mix(in srgb, var(--color-line-soft) 25%, transparent);
```

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What does it paint? | A uniform wash across the pad face, **behind the canvas**. Lit cells are opaque and cover it; unlit cells are written at alpha 0 and let it through; Layer 3's black gutters then cut it into 81 squares |
| What colour is it? | `--color-line-soft` (rgb 214 255 78 / 0.2) at 25% -> **0.05**. **No new token, no new hex, no fourth hue** - the value names the existing token and nothing else, so `identity.spec.ts`'s nine tokens and three hexes are untouched and every colour gate still reads a `var()` |
| Why `color-mix` and not a pseudo-element with `opacity`? | One declaration instead of five; no fifth layer in a component whose header contracts four; no `opacity < 1` on thirty-six elements, which is a paint-time group per pad. `color-mix` is Chrome 111+, Firefox 113+, Safari 16.2+ and was **rendered and screenshotted in chromium before this plan was written** |
| Is it stronger than an unlit cell elsewhere? | **No, and that is the constraint.** 0.05 is one quarter of the dot's own 0.2, so the dot stays the brightest mark in every unlit cell and the wash is strictly dimmer at every pixel. Its cell-area average (0.05) sits above the dot's cell-area average (~0.026) and far below the dot's peak |
| Why global rather than scoped to `tpad`? | The brief's own words: *the cell structure at the same strength the other cards' unlit cells have*. Scoping it to Trackpad would make Trackpad's unlit cells **brighter** than everyone's, which is the one thing that phrase forbids. Global means parity is by construction and there is no entry-specific branch to explain |
| Does the OG image change? | **No.** `scripts/gen-og.mjs` renders from the engine in Node with no CSS at all, so Trackpad's OG image stays black - which is consistent, because the config really does write no LED |

**Measured before this plan was written**, at `deviceScaleFactor: 2` against the built site: at 0.04,
0.06 and via the shipped `color-mix` form, the Trackpad card renders as a legible 9x9 grid of dark
lamps, and the HOLD card's lit frame still dominates its now-legible unlit interior. Screenshots are
in the scratchpad and the before/after pair goes in the SUMMARY.

## Why a source scan cannot close this, and what does

**Warning 1 of this phase, in its own words:** *the lattice already shipped once painting nothing with
every source scan green - two screenshots came back byte-identical.* The cause then was `isolation`
and paint order. The cause here is **occlusion**, which is also paint order, and which a text scan of
`src/app.css` is equally blind to: a scan can prove `.lattice > :where(*)` is declared and it cannot
prove that anything is occluded by it, or that the grid still shows through, or that the wash on an
unlit cell is brighter than the ground.

So this plan ships **two source scans and one browser gate**, and the browser gate is the point:

| Gate | File | What only it can see |
|---|---|---|
| scan 6 | `src/lib/ui/instrument.spec.ts` (5 -> **6**) | The rule exists once, at `:where()` specificity, in `src/app.css` and nowhere else; the three exceptions are declared in the three named files with the three named values; neither lattice root's own component authors the ground rule locally |
| scan 8 | `src/lib/ui/aesthetic.spec.ts` (7 -> **8**) | The wash names exactly one `var(--color-*)` and it is `--color-line-soft`; there is no colour literal in it; its mix percentage is at most 50 so it cannot exceed the dot; `.dots` declares nothing that moves; and `DARK_BY_CONSTRUCTION` still names `tpad` with `demoPathFor("tpad")` still undefined |
| test 5 | `e2e/aesthetic.e2e.ts` (4 -> **5** titles, `@webkit`) | **That it is actually true in a browser.** Every text-bearing element under a lattice root has an opaque ancestor before the root; the grid gutters do not; and an unlit pad cell is measurably brighter than `--color-ground` while `tpad`'s canvas still reports zero lit bytes |

**Test 5 carries `@webkit` and that costs a title in both projects (+2, not +1).** The reason is

exactly the kind of thing that can differ, and the phone viewport is the surface most visitors who
cannot install will see.
