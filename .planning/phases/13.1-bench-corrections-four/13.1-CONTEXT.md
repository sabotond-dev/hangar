## D-01 [user] The intro fits the screen — no page scroll

> *"I dont want the index page to be scrollable, always fit on the screen"*

Bench line 1. `/` renders inside the viewport with no document scroll at every desktop viewport —
the wide and compact bands (1024 and above), measured at 1920 x 1080, 1440 x 900, 1366 x 768 and the
harness's own 1280 x 720. The intro becomes height-aware: the hero's square is the smaller of the
column's width and the height left after the strip; the words column's spacing and the headline's
size clamp against `dvh`; the strip stays at the foot. **On a phone the page may scroll**: 13-07
stacked the surface below the words (deferred-items D.10, open), and the user's rule is about the
desktop. `app.css`'s `.type-display` role is untouched (D-17 is the PDF's scale); the intro clamps
its own headline locally. **The document-height assertion is an e2e title in chromium at the four
viewports, not a source scan** — a stylesheet can promise a fit it does not deliver.

## D-03 [user] Sandbox elements resize by dragging their handles; each has its own colour; a fuller look

> *"in sandbox: you should be able to resize each element by draggin its points. it has visible points
> but they cannot be resized by that as of now. also, each element should have its own color and
> better visualization, now its too minimal."*

Bench line 3. Three things.

**Drag-resize.** 13-16 built the eight handles as inert SVG `<rect>`s and wrote *"a drag is the
accelerator and nothing more"*. Now a pointer down on a handle starts a resize: while the pointer
moves, the PROPOSED bounds (the dashed rectangle 13-16 already draws) follow the cell under the
pointer, snapped to the lattice; on release the box goes through the editor — one new method,
`resizeSelectedTo(box)` — and through `geometry.ts`'s `applyEdit` like every other edit, so an
off-surface, overlapping or too-small box is REFUSED and the region stays as it was, with the
refusal in the status line (section 8: "snap visibly; show proposed bounds before committing";
"preserve the previous valid value when an edit is invalid"). One history entry per drag (the
history's coalesce key, sealed on release). **13-16's rule that no drag is ever required stands**: the
numeric fields and the keyboard route are unchanged, and `sandbox-ui.spec.ts` test 2's "no
pointer-move" clause is inverted honestly — a move during a handle drag draws the proposal and
commits nothing.

**Own colour.** Every new element takes the next colour from a four-entry palette on the RGB444
lattice, cycling by creation order — the PDF's page 3 gives two (Filter's lime `13,15,7`, Space's
teal, measured off the page), and the plan proposes two more in the same key; the picker changes
nothing and a visitor can still recolour. **The third and fourth colours are proposed, not decided —
a bench row asks.** The cost is unmoved: `emit.spec.ts` measures every colour at the picker corner
already (G.43).

**A fuller look — read off PDF page 3, not invented.** The tint denser than 13-16's 0.18 (measured
off the page); a Fader draws its track and a wide thumb; an XY pad its crosshair and a dot; a Button
its `OFF` / `ON` chip below the name; a Knob its circle with the pointer and the name centred beneath;
the 1px boundary and the 11px uppercase name stay. The Knob's circle stays an SVG `<circle>` (13-16:
a true circle by construction, not a radius). Where the PDF's mark is ambiguous the executor keeps
13-16's mark and names the ambiguity for the bench row.

## D-07 [user] Put back is removed

> *"we dont even need the Put back function that totally unnecessary if we have a clear button."*

Bench line 6, and the user's answer on 2026-09-12 to the orchestrator's question — *"Clear writes the
firmware default; Put back restores your own configuration — without it the only way back after a
TRY is Grid Editor. remove / keep?"* — **"remove"**.

**The consequence, plainly:** after an Apply, the way back to the user's own page is Grid Editor or a
Clear to the firmware default; HANGAR no longer offers to put the module's own configuration back.
`Put back` leaves the interface everywhere — the workspace's zone, the Sandbox's zone, and it was
never under Device actions. **What stays:** the snapshot machinery whole (`snapshot.ts`, the fetch at
connect before any write, `hangar.snapshot.v4` and the older keys read and flagged, the per-page
record, the re-snapshot on a page change) — SAFE-03's snapshot-before-write is a safety feature, not a
control; the store's `putBack()` action and the `restoring` / `restored` phases, reachable from the
`/dev/install/` probe only (the probe keeps its button; `install.spec.ts` and the probe's e2e titles
keep proving the restore path so the machinery cannot rot); J.40's substitution of the snapshot's own
255/4 for a landing's empty utility (owed, unchanged by this). **What the gate amends by the user's
word rather than ticks:** the requirement rows that name `PUT BACK` as a control — **SAFE-03** (*"and
`PUT BACK` restores them with one click at any time"*), **SAFE-04** (*"so `PUT BACK` survives a
closed tab"*), **SAFE-09** (*"with `PUT BACK` still offered"*) — and **the "always recoverable"
wording**, which is PROJECT.md's Safety constraint (*"the module's original config is always
recoverable"*, `.planning/PROJECT.md:114`, mirrored in `CLAUDE.md`) rather than a requirement row;
SAFE-01 is qualified (five clicks, the fifth now the select; `Put back` no longer among them —
`WRITE_CLICKS` drops to four members: Apply, Store, Clear, Target). Every string that offered Put
back as the way back — `STEP_OR_PUT_BACK`, `CONFIRM_WAY_BACK`, `identifiedBody`, `clearedBody`, the
