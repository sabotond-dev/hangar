<objective>
D-07 is answered. This plan reads the answer, works out what it costs, and asks the one question the
answer opened.

**The probe was run on the user's ZONA on 2026-09-10** (`SLOT-PROBE.md`, result in
`PROBE-RESULTS-2026-09-10.md`, committed at `493a21a`). The recorded observation is
*"bottom right corner lits up!"* - **cell 80**, which the probe's own table maps to:

1. **An event body is a callable method on its element** (`ele[N].name = function(self)…`,
   `grid_ui.c:373`), reachable from another event's body; and
2. **globals defined inside it persist** once it has run.

So **the touch Timer is a library slot HANGAR already writes**, and the touch Setup can pull it in with
an eleven-character call. **D-07's condition is met and the split architecture is on.** Nothing in this
plan re-asks it, and `13-RESEARCH.md`'s "unverified" rows for `self:tim()` are **superseded by
observation** - the research was written before the probe ran (`a61e74a` precedes `eaac143`), and
13-VALIDATION records that as a correction rather than leaving two documents disagreeing.

**What the probe did NOT prove, quoted from its own results rather than softened:**

- **`ele[1]:map()` - the system element's mapmode slot - was not tested.** The probe wrote only the two
  touch events HANGAR already writes. The mechanism is the same so it is *likely*, but it is unverified,
  **and event 4 doubles as the utility button** (its firmware default is page-next). That is a second
  bench row if a plan wants a third slot.
- Whether a body called this way runs with the **same `self`** it would have on its own schedule was not
  distinguished from "it ran at all".
- **The Timer was never armed.** A library Timer that is also scheduled re-runs its definitions on every
  tick - harmless for pure definitions, a cost only if the body does work. **The Sandbox's Timer will do
  work**: Phase 12's expiry sweep lives there.

**And here is the arithmetic that makes this plan necessary rather than a formality.** The research
measured the generic runtime `Z` at **861** with the **Knob left explicitly undefined** (its own table
says *"type 5 — undefined"*). **D-08 then chose a real rotary gesture at an estimated +150 to +200.**
So a runtime carrying all four element kinds is roughly **1,011 to 1,061** - and the only slot the probe
proved, the touch Timer, must also carry Phase 12's expiry sweep call. **The four-kind surface the PDF
itself draws - a Fader, an XY pad, a Button and a Knob - does not fit in the two slots that are proved.**

**Dead-branch elimination is what makes this a scale question rather than a wall**, and it applies to the
runtime as well as to the data, **because HANGAR emits the runtime per surface**. A surface of faders
and buttons needs two branches; the PDF's needs four. So the ceiling is not "the Sandbox fits" or "it
does not" - it is **how many kinds one surface may mix**, and the third slot buys 908 more.

**That is the question this plan asks**, with the numbers. **It is the only question it asks.** The
action colour used to be the checkpoint's second half; **D-16 answered it after the plan-check** - the
Bible's `#DCFF71`, not HANGAR's `#D6FF4E` - so 13-03 writes its eleven tokens against a decision that
already exists and this plan neither offers it nor re-opens it.

**Where these six figures come from, stated before anybody leans on them.** The research's `861` and
its per-kind breakdown are read off **`13-RESEARCH.md` §3.1, a sketch that was never RUN** - it was
costed under the minifier and reasoned about, and its own table leaves the Knob as *"type 5 -
undefined"*. That is the exact failure mode Phase 12 caught in its own sketch and named, and it is the
lesson 13-15's objective quotes. **So this plan's six figures are a ceiling estimate taken from an
unrun sketch, and they are labelled as one everywhere they appear** - in `SLOT-ARITHMETIC.md`, in the
checkpoint, and in the SUMMARY. **13-15 measures the real runtime in a real Lua VM** (wasmoon) with
D-08's rotary written out, and **13-15's figure supersedes every figure here.** The checkpoint says so
to the user's face, and 13-15 carries a re-ask if its measured ceiling lands below the one the user
decided against.

Purpose: the answered probe turned into a budget, and the one question it opened put to the user.
Output: one arithmetic document, one runbook row, one checkpoint, `+0 / +0`.

carry**, and both go in the row:
