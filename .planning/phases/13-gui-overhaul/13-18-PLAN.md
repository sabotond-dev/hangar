<objective>
The words, decided by the person whose product it is.

**The scale, counted rather than estimated** (`grep -cE "^export (const|function) "`):
`install-copy.ts` **59** exports in 545 lines, `session-copy.ts` **47** in 554, `tune/copy.ts` **56** in
532 - **162 user-facing strings or string functions**, plus 27 `quiet` lines and 27 descriptions in the
catalog. Of those, **about 35 have a §16 row or a PDF line**, **about 40 need a new line in the same
register**, and the rest are HANGAR mechanics the Bible never saw - the meters, the ladder, the picker,
the identity sentences.

**This plan owns the two device modules** (106 of the 162) and **the batch**. 13-19 owns `tune/copy.ts`,
the catalog copy and the twenty-seven names.

**Why the batch is one stop and not forty guesses.** D-05 says: *"§16's table is the reference for
each; where a state has no line there, write one in the same register and flag it for the user."*
Seventeen waves have each written the strings they needed and appended them to `13-COPY-NEW.md` rather
than inventing them twice. **This is where the ledger is emptied.** Forty strings in one table, each
with the state it names and the fact it must carry, is a ten-minute read; forty strings spread across
nineteen SUMMARYs is not reviewable at all.

**The one place §16 must not be followed.** It offers a single row - *"The device stopped responding.
Your draft is safe; device state could not be verified."* - for **six** distinct HANGAR outcomes:
`unconfirmed`, `kept-mismatch`, `partial`, `nothing-landed`, `restored-unconfirmed` and
`snapshot-failed`. Each is a **measured** state with a different recovery, and 13-11 kept them apart in
the interface for exactly that reason. **Six lines, in the batch, and the reason is stated in the batch
itself**, so the user is choosing six sentences knowingly rather than being handed a collapse.

**What the honesty caps were, and why they go by name.** Phase 10's copy specs assert **measured maximum
lengths** per string, so a caption could not outgrow its box. D-05 supersedes them: the register is
sentence-case and second-person, the boxes are the Bible's, and a cap measured against Phase 10's
layout is a cap against a layout that no longer exists. **They are retired by name, with the sentence
that says what replaced them** - the Bible's own geometry and the fact that the shell's regions are now
proportional. The *other* rules those specs assert - real apostrophes, no exclamation marks, no emoji,
no uppercase paragraphs, no "Error", no browser engine named, no control label paraphrased in prose -
**all carry over and are re-asserted against the new strings.**

**Four facts must survive the change of words**, and each is traced from its old string to its new one
in a table: **RAM against flash**; **the snapshot**; **the firmware default**; and **nothing is written
without a click**. `SAFE_NOTE` is Phase 10's wording of the fourth and it has no §16 row - so it is in
the batch, and its *fact* is non-negotiable whatever sentence the user picks.

Purpose: the product's voice, chosen once, by its owner.
Output: one batch answered, two modules rewritten, two specs rewritten with their counts held. Term
`+0 / +0`.
</objective>

## The register rules that survive, and are re-asserted

Real apostrophes (`you're`, `you've`) - **and the Bible uses them, so this is now a positive test rather
than a prohibition**; no exclamation marks; no emoji; no uppercase paragraphs; no bare "Error"; **no
browser engine named** (`CONN-02`'s own rule - the unsupported message names Chrome, Edge and desktop
Firefox 151+, never "Chromium"); and **no control label paraphrased in prose**, which is what stops the
body text from saying "press the try button" when the button says something else.

**`DisabledReason` stays a union.** It is the mechanism that forbids a disabled control without a named
reason, it is stronger than §7's own requirement, and 13-11 relied on it. Re-word its members; do not
loosen its type.
