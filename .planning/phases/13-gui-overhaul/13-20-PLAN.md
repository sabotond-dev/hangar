  <what-built>
The interface is a different application. There is a header with your wordmark and three sections -
Playground, Sandbox, My configs - a context bar that says in one line what your draft is and what your
ZONA is, a rail, a workspace and an inspector at the proportions your mockup drew. The texture is gone:
no CRT, no halftone, no lattice, no splash, no coverflow. Text is near-white on warm graphite and the
acid colour now means one thing - the action you are about to take, and the thing that is selected.
**There is not a single rounded corner**, held by three tests: one that reads the source, one that reads
the built stylesheet, and one that measures every element in a real browser - which is the only place
the browser's own default radii on a search field or a select can be seen. The three genuine circles in
the colour picker are still round, and the browser proves each of their boxes is square.

**Two things are new rather than re-skinned.** The **Sandbox**: you place faders, buttons, knobs and XY
pads on the 9x9 with clicks or with numbers, and it compiles to real Lua and installs to your ZONA
through exactly the same writer, snapshot and acknowledgements as everything else - there is no second
path to your hardware, and a test asserts there is not. And **the page target**: the dropdown in the
context bar now moves your module's active page, behind a confirmation that names both pages, gated on
your module's own report of where it landed.

**Nothing in any of it has touched a ZONA.** Every claim was measured in a real Lua VM, in the
simulator, against the fake module, or read out of firmware C. Two of the decisions this phase made are
things only your fingers can judge, and they are the first two rows below.
