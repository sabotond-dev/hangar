**But D-03 was made without a fact nobody had, and the fact blocks the plan.**

`11-RESEARCH.md` says, of the removal: *"The eight-entry ring itself is untouched — it is the eight

the tree:

```
FRONT_DOOR = aurora, pinwheel, ninepads, starfield, joystick, RADAR, faders, dial
front-door.spec.ts:110-112 — every ring member must have preview === "padsim"
```

**RADAR is ring position 6 of 8.** Its description is even the shipped precedent this phase borrowed
twice — *"Rings roll out from the centre, and the pad sends your finger's position to your computer."*

Delivering the ask is **not descriptor-reachable**: RADAR is a compiler-driven card whose `ripple`
look plus `xy` sends cannot express user-placed points at all. Delivering it means a hand-authored Lua
entry, whose `preview` is `"lua"`, which **fails the ring's own requirement** and takes the ring to
seven — breaking the documented geometry that keeps the three quiet pads at positions 2, 4 and 6 so no
two dead squares are ever side by side.

That is a product decision the user has not been asked, and this plan opens with it rather than
picking one.

Purpose: the RADAR ask, delivered in whichever shape the user chooses, with the front door left in a
state somebody decided.
Output: one entry, one ring decision, and a catalog count that did not move.

## The fact that blocks the plan

`front-door.ts`'s header states the ring's property in its own words: **the three quiet pads land on
2, 4 and 6, so no two of them are ever side by side, including across the wrap.** The ring is eight
because it is the nine presets minus `tpad` (which is `exclusive`), and `front-door.spec.ts:110-112`
requires `preview === "padsim"` of every member with the comment *"'lua' entries have no simulator
engine until plan 08-03 lands one, so they stay out of the row rather than rendering as a hole."*

**Note that comment is itself stale** — 08-03 did land a Lua engine, and `preview === "lua"` entries
animate perfectly well in browse. **Check whether the requirement is still load-bearing or merely
historical before treating it as a wall**, and report the answer either way. That single check may
turn a three-option product decision into a one-line spec amendment, and it is the first thing this
plan should establish.

`10-07`'s deferred item already records that growing the ring is blocked by the same requirement,
which twenty-seven of thirty-six entries failed. **This plan is the first to have a reason to test
whether that block is real.**

## If a hand-authored RADAR is built

The ask, decomposed: **a sweep**, **points a user can add and remove**, **multiple active points**, and
**note-on and note-off as the sweep crosses each**.

**SONAR is the shipped implementation of almost exactly that**, and D-03 says so plainly. So:

- **Read `sonar.ts` first and reuse its structure openly**, rather than deriving a parallel one. Its
  arm-a-cell gesture now accepts a swipe (11-08), its centre is lit, and its notes are released. A
  RADAR that repeats those decisions is honest; a RADAR that solves them differently for no reason is
  the "quiet differentiation" D-03 forbids.
- **The one difference that is legitimate is geometric and it is in the two names.** SONAR sweeps an
  angle; a radar is rings rolling out from the centre. If the sweep is radial rather than angular,
  the two cards genuinely differ in what they do to a given arrangement of points, and that difference
  came from the geometry rather than from a planner's taste. **State whether you took it and why.**
- **Budget**: SONAR is 432 Setup / 279 Timer, so the shape is known to be affordable. Cost RADAR's own
  before writing it.
- Both 11-02 gates apply; the decay for a hit point goes through the rescued idiom.

**The preset does not disappear in options (a) or (d).** `presets.ts` keeps nine — HANGAR owns the
values and `presets.spec.ts` test 3 asserts the same nine — and `preset-baseline.json` and
`golden-frames.json` keep measuring all nine. What changes is which **catalog entry** the `radar` id
resolves to: `ported.ts` loses a member (9 → 8) and `index.ts` gains a Lua entry. **The catalog count
does not move**, and the SUMMARY says that in those words, because "we replaced a preset entry with a
Lua entry" and "we removed a card" look identical in a diff and are completely different facts.
