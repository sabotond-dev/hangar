<objective>
CONSOLE drew three separate complaints from the bench, more than any other entry, and it is the
**second-tightest Setup in the catalog** — 785 of 908, **121 free at the worst knob position**. That
combination is why it leads this plan.

**And CONSOLE is the precedent that gives this plan its other two cards.** *"Not precise"* sounded
like an event-handling complaint and turned out to be a real arithmetic ceiling — `h*127//8` topping
out at 111 of 127 — found by **sweeping a column through the real Lua host and reading the emitted
values**. Two other entries drew a complaint of exactly that shape and neither was ever swept:

> FORGE: *"waay not precise enough, make it much more precise"*
> LATTICE: *"not precise enough, make it more precise, **clamp it better**"*

Task 03 applies CONSOLE's method to both. **It does not carry a fix**, because inventing one before
the sweep is what CONSOLE's diagnosis would have got wrong.

> CONSOLE: *"should react to touch or swiping as well not just pushing, also clamp issue in the top row
> because its not precise. you should not be able to interact with the 'muted' faders."*

**The clamp complaint has a named cause and it is arithmetic, not event handling.** Sweeping a finger
down column 4 through the real Lua host emits exactly eight values:

```
0, 15, 31, 47, 63, 79, 95, 111        (controller 20)
```

Row 0 is the mute strip, so `r` runs 1..8, `h = 8 - r` runs 7..0, and the emitted value is
`h*127//8`, whose maximum is `7*127//8 = 111`. **The mute row owns the top of the fader's travel, so
the fader has eight steps and cannot reach the top.** Both readings of *"clamp issue in the top row
because its not precise"* land on that same fact.

**`h*127//8` → `h*127//7` is +0 characters** — both literals are one digit — and it makes the eight
steps span 0..127 exactly.

**The roadmap groups CONSOLE with the swipe family and that grouping is wrong.** CONSOLE already ships
`if e~=1 and e~=4 and e<9 then return end`: it accepts MOVE today and its fader body already tracks a
swipe. **Only its mute row is onset-gated** (`if e==4 or e>8`). That is a much smaller change than the
per-contact cell guard EUCLID, SONAR and STEPS need in 11-08, and conflating them would spend
characters CONSOLE does not have.

Purpose: three bench complaints on the catalog's second-tightest card, two of them for five characters
between them.
Output: one entry edited, two behaviour tests, and a measured margin.
</objective>

**CONSOLE as shipped**, `console.ts:130`, Setup only, no Timer, 785 of 908:

```lua
self.touch_cb=function(s,i,e,x,y)
  if e~=1 and e~=4 and e<9 then return end
  local c=x*9//128 local r=y*9//128
  if r==0 then
    if e==4 or e>8 then
      local m=not s.m[c] s.m[c]=m
      s:gms(@CH,176,@CC+c,m and 0 or s.v[c]*127//8,0)
      P(s,c)
    end
    return
  end
  local h=8-r
  if h~=s.v[c] or s.m[c] then
    s.v[c]=h s.m[c]=nil s:gms(@CH,176,@CC+c,h*127//8,0) P(s,c)
  end
end
```

Read the whole file, including `P(s,c)`'s two-layer paint, before touching any of it.

**The three asks, costed:**

| Ask | Change | delta | free after, at defaults | **free after, at worst** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full scale | `h*127//8` → `h*127//7`, **at both sites** — the fader body and the unmute restore | **+0** | 123 | **121** |
| Muted faders inert | the fader body returns early when `s.m[c]` is set | **+5** | 118 | **116** |
| The mute row takes a swipe | `if e==4 or e>8` in the `r==0` branch relaxed to accept MOVE | measure | — | — |

**"Free" means two different things and they must never share a heading.** *At defaults* is
`908 − cost at the shipped knob values`; *at worst* is `908 − cost at the worst reachable knob
position`. CONSOLE measures **785 at defaults and 787 at worst**, so the two differ by 2 here.
**The binding gate is worst-based** — `lua-entries.sweep.spec.ts` asserts every entry inside 908 at
every knob position — so **every margin this plan states, in prose and in its SUMMARY, is stated at
worst**, and the sentence below about "CONSOLE's 121 characters" is the worst-based figure before any
of the three asks is spent.

**Two sites, not one.** `h*127//8` appears in the fader body **and** inside the mute branch, where the
unmute restores the fader's stored value. Changing one and not the other means an unmute jumps to a
different value than the fader shows. **Grep the file rather than trusting this sentence.**

**"You should not be able to interact with the muted faders" is a real behaviour change and it
conflicts with what the card does today.** As shipped, touching a muted fader **unmutes it**:
`s.m[c]=nil` in the fader body. That is a shipped design decision — a fader you touch is a fader you
want — and the user is overriding it. Two consequences:

1. **Once muted faders are inert, the only way to unmute is the mute row.** Confirm that is reachable
   and obvious before shipping it, because a mute you cannot undo except from one specific row is
   worse than the complaint. The mute row is row 0 of the same column, so it is reachable; **say so
   with the geometry rather than assuming it.**
2. The `+5` figure assumes the early return replaces the `s.m[c]=nil` clear. **Measure it.**

**The mute row and the swipe.** The `r==0` branch gates on onset (`e==4 or e>8`) because it
**toggles**: accepting MOVE without a guard would toggle the mute at 100 Hz while a finger rests on
it. Two honest shapes, and the plan must choose one and say why:

- **A per-contact last-cell guard**, the same shape 11-08 gives EUCLID: remember the last mute cell
  each contact toggled and ignore repeats. Correct, costs the most, and is the same idiom as the
  neighbouring plan.
- **A swipe that SETS rather than TOGGLES** — dragging across the mute row mutes everything it
  crosses, and the toggle stays on the press. Cheaper, and arguably what a mixer's mute row should do,
  but it is a **different behaviour** from what the user asked and must be presented as a choice
  rather than shipped as an interpretation.

**Cost both before choosing.** CONSOLE has 118 free after the first two asks, and the per-contact
guard on EUCLID cost 76. **If the guard does not fit, that is a finding**: report the shortfall, state
which shape does fit, and let the SUMMARY carry the decision to 11-16 rather than trimming the ask.

**One thing that is NOT the answer, and it is the obvious wrong one.** *"Not precise enough"* on a
cell-grid entry is never a coordinate-resolution problem. Firmware reports 0..127 by dividing the
native 0..1023 by 8; for a nine-cell grid each column is 14.22 units wide, so 7-bit quantisation
determines a column boundary to within about **0.07 of a cell**. Unlocking 10 bits with
`self:txma(1023)self:tyma(1023)` improves that to 0.009 of a cell — imperceptible — and costs **+30**.
**Do not spend CONSOLE's 121 characters on it.** It matters only for continuous output, which is why
STRIP uses it and no cell-grid card does.

**The value sweep is the test.** Drive a finger down each column through the real Lua host and collect
the emitted controller values. Before: eight values topping out at 111. After: eight values spanning
0..127 with **127 present**. Assert the presence of 127 and the count of distinct values, not the
whole list — a list assertion would go red on any future re-scaling for no reason.

## FORGE and LATTICE: the same method, on two complaints nothing has answered

**FORGE's bench note reaches no plan in this phase, no removal and no deferral.** Across the sixteen
plans the word appears twice and neither is work: 11-02 names FORGE as an entry that **already** writes
the touch guard correctly, and 11-16's non-delivery list does not include it. `11-RESEARCH.md:599`
rules out the *obvious* fix — *"do not spend budget on `txma`/`tyma` for LATTICE, EUCLID, FORGE or
CONSOLE expecting it to answer the complaint"* — and proposes nothing in its place. **A phase that
closes reporting thirty-five notes answered with one silently dropped is exactly what its own gates
exist to prevent.**

**LATTICE's note has three clauses and two are answered.** *"not precise enough, make it more
precise"* is the fast tap, fixed in 11-02 at +8 — LATTICE emitted **0 messages on a fast tap against 2
on a slow one**, which is a defensible reading of "not precise enough". ***"clamp it better" is the
third clause and nothing has looked at it.*** CONSOLE's clamp complaint sounded identical and was a
real ceiling, so an assertion that the fast-tap fix covered this one is not evidence.

**The budgets are not the constraint.** FORGE is **189 free at worst** in Setup (716 at defaults, 719
at worst) and **532 free at worst** in Timer. LATTICE is **291 free at worst** in Setup before 11-02's
+8, so **283 after**.

**What to sweep, and what a finding looks like.** This is a measurement task and the deliverable is
numbers either way. Do not design a fix before the numbers exist.

- **FORGE** sends one `gks` per press; the macro index is `@KEY0 + r//3*9 + c` with `c = x*9//128`
  and `r = y*9//128`. Sweep **x across 0..127 at several y, and y across 0..127 at several x**, and
  read the recorded HID out of the host. Report: the **set of macro indices actually reachable** out
  of the twenty-seven the header claims; **where each column and band boundary falls in raw
  coordinates**, and how wide and tall each target is; and whether any index is reachable over a
  smaller area than its neighbours. Cell 80 is the bank corner and its branch returns before the send,
  so **macro 26's strip is two rows rather than three** — confirm that, measure it, and state whether
  it is the complaint or merely a fact.
- **FORGE's onset-only gate is the second thing to measure, not to assume.** The handler reads
  `if e~=4 and e<9 then return end`, so a press is final: a finger that lands one column off and
  slides to the intended macro sends the wrong macro and never corrects. **Measure it** — drive a
  down-then-move gesture that crosses a boundary and count what was sent. That is a plausible
  mechanism for *"waay not precise"* on a labelless twenty-seven-target pad, **and it is also exactly
  the kind of thing a macro pad must not "fix" by firing on MOVE**, which would spray keystrokes. If
  the sweep shows it, the finding is the finding; the fix is a separate decision with its own cost.
- **LATTICE** is a clamp complaint, so sweep what it clamps. Read the file first, then drive the full
  travel on both axes through the real Lua host and report **the emitted range against the range the
  card claims**: does it reach both ends, is any `glim` or `//` truncating a reachable value away,
  and does the picture's cell boundary sit where the emitted value's step does. CONSOLE's defect was
  visible the moment the emitted set was printed; print LATTICE's.

**Then one of two outcomes per card, and both are deliverables:**

1. **A defect the numbers name.** Fix it, cost it before and after at the worst knob position, and say
   what the sweep showed.
2. **Measured and clean.** Write the numbers into the SUMMARY and hand them to 11-16 as a **named
   non-delivery with its measurement**, not as a silence. *"Swept the whole travel, all twenty-seven
   macro indices reachable, boundaries at these coordinates, nothing found"* is an answer. Nothing is
   not.

**The sweep is the test in either case.** CONSOLE's precedent: the measurement that found the defect
becomes the assertion that stops it returning. A sweep that found nothing still pins the arithmetic it
swept, which is worth exactly as much the next time somebody re-cuts a coordinate expression.
