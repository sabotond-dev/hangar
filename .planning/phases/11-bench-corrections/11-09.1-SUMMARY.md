## Task 01 - ARC stops when you tap its heart (`907a1bd`)

### The sentence this plan exists to be able to write: THE SIMULATOR WAS NEVER WRONG

The bench note read *"if you press the center it stops, pressing it again
resumes, not intuitive enough"*, and `arc.ts` had **no stop, no resume and no
toggle of any kind**: `s.r = 1 + x*31//127` is at least 1 at every x, so the
oscillator could not reach rate 0 by any gesture the card admits. The open
question was whether the hardware did something the preview does not reproduce
- **which would have meant HANGAR was showing a catalogue card as something it
is not.** The user's answer, *"i meant to add stopping and resuming tap as a
feature"*, closes that: they were describing an intention, not an observation.
**Nothing about the simulator is in doubt, and a whole class of worry closes
rather than being carried into 11-16.** The sentence is in the entry header as
well as here.

### The gesture, costed three ways, and the hole the chosen one leaves in real values

| Candidate | Verdict | The hole a press STARTING there leaves |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) cell 40, the middle pixel of the 3x3 heart** | **CHOSEN** | raw x and y in 57..71 map to it, so rate **14..18** (of 1..32) at depth **56..70** (of 0..127) - the middle of both ranges |
| (b) the whole 3x3 heart block | rejected | nine times bigger: rate **11..21** at depth **42..84**, exactly where a user aiming for "medium rate, medium depth" presses |
| (c) a second contact | rejected | no hole, but invisible in the preview - see below |

**The hole is halved by the onset gating and that is asserted, not claimed.**
The toggle fires only on `e==4 or e>8`, so a finger that presses anywhere else
and **drags through** the centre still sets rate and depth. `lua-smoke.spec.ts`
drives exactly that stroke and reads back swirl rate 15 with 50 distinct
controller values.

**Candidate (c) is rejected on 11-09's measurement rather than on a fresh one,
and the plan's stated gate would have admitted it.** The plan asked that (c) be
gated on "first proving `i>0` is deliverable at all through the Lua host and the
shipped `TouchSampler`". It **is** deliverable - 11-09 measured `touch_cb`
seeing `i = 0, 1, 2` and `touch.ts` allocating three slots. It fails one step
downstream: `src/lib/ui/Coverflow.svelte`'s `onHeroDown` maps one `pointerId` to
one contact, so **a visitor driving the preview with a mouse can never perform
the gesture**, which is the failure `PREV-01` exists to prevent. Confirmed on
this tree by reading the six `pointerId` sites in `Coverflow.svelte`.

### The stopped picture, costed twice, with the freeze-to-black question measured

| Candidate | Verdict | The measurement |
|---|---|---|
| (i) the heart freezes alone | **rejected** | across 60 consecutive stop moments at full depth the frozen heart lands anywhere in **0..254** and **below 16 on 2 of the 60**. Black is reachable, so a stopped card would sometimes be indistinguishable from a dead one |
| **(ii) the heart freezes AND the swirl stops** | **CHOSEN** | `glf` is a rate-only setter, so rate 0 holds the swirl's picture rather than blanking it: the **brightest frozen cell is 253 of 255**. A stopped ARC is a still, plainly lit pad |

Cost against ARC's 518 free Setup: the whole feature is **+133**, of which the
81-write stop/resume paint is the larger part. It reuses the shape the
rate-change branch already performs, through a Setup-local `local function F(f)`
captured by `touch_cb` - the same idiom `stage.ts`'s `Z(z,f,g)` established, and
worth about 25 characters against duplicating the loop.

### Three implementation details that are not decoration

- **`self.s` is a separate flag and the Timer multiplies the phase step by it**
  (`s.h + s.r*s.s`). Stopping by zeroing `s.r` cannot work: the touch handler
  recomputes `r = 1 + x*31//127` on every accepted sample. Negative check 2
  proves it, below.
- **`self.f` carries the swirl rate so the resume is exact.** Setup arms layer 2
  at rate 4 while the touch path writes `glim(r//2,1,120)`, which is 2 at the
  default `r = 4` - so a resume that recomputed would hand the card back
  slightly slower than it left. `F(s.f)` restores what was actually written.
- **The keeper re-arm gained `or s.s<1`, and it is load-bearing.** The keeper
  rides on `p<s.r`, true exactly on the phase wrap - and a stopped card has no
  wrap, so **655 seconds after a stop the LED engine would have expired layer 2
  and the frozen swirl would have gone out.** A zero rate together with a keeper
  is the legitimate form, not pitfall 1: `decay-idiom.spec.ts` is green on it and
  the pitfall's signature is a keeper together with a decay at or above 200.

### The repeat-traffic decision, with the number said out loud

**A stopped ARC emits 50 MIDI messages a second, all carrying the same frozen
value, and that is deliberate.** Suppressing it the way `morph.ts` suppresses an
unmoved corner was considered and rejected for three reasons:

1. **MORPH's shouting was four DIFFERENT CCs racing each other for a MIDI-learn
   binding. ARC has exactly one CC**, so repeating it is what makes it
   *learnable* rather than what makes it unreachable. The precedent does not
   transfer.
2. MIDI is stateful, so a host that connects mid-stop learns the held value at
   once instead of waiting for a resume.
3. **A stop that went silent would be indistinguishable from a Timer that
   raised**, and the smoke gate's non-vacuity clause would have nothing left to
   stand on.

A general last-value guard is additionally **not available here at any price**:
at depth 0 the controller is a constant 64 by design, and 11-09's amplitude test
asserts that ARC still sends there over 200 messages.

### The test, and why it drives BOTH arrival shapes

`lua-smoke.spec.ts` 14 -> 15: *"stops ARC on a centre tap and resumes it on the
next, once per press"*. It asserts an ordered sequence of emitted CC values
across a scripted tap-wait-tap, with **the flat stretch pinned by its LENGTH as
well as by its constancy**, so a one-sample plateau cannot pass. Observed:

```
slow: DOWN then UP     running [50 msg, 50 distinct, 4..126] -> stopped [50 msg, 1 distinct, 55..55] -> resumed [50 msg, 50 distinct, 0..126]; swirl rate 0 while stopped, 4 after
fast: coalesced DOWNUP running [50 msg, 50 distinct, 4..126] -> stopped [50 msg, 1 distinct, 55..55] -> resumed [50 msg, 50 distinct, 0..126]; swirl rate 0 while stopped, 4 after
drag THROUGH the centre: swirl rate 15, 50 msg, 50 distinct
```

**The fast tap is proved specifically, and it had to be synthesised to be
proved.** The browser cannot make one (see the finding below), so `touchTap`
drives the coalesced DOWNUP directly. **One fast tap stops OR resumes, never
both.**

One probe hazard worth recording because it bit during the work: the host's
`enqueue` is change-gated on `(event, x, y)` per contact, so **two identical
taps at one pixel are silently deduped by the HOST and read as "the resume does
not work"**. The probe moves inside cell 40 between taps (60,60 then 65,65) and
asserts both land on cell 40.

## Task 02 - a MORPH corner tap speaks for that corner and nothing else (`a81f3e3`)

### The fourth reading, and why it is cheaper than the three the checkpoint costed

The plan's richest option was an assignment mode - a latch, a selection gesture
and a single-corner emit - and it named **the gesture as the expensive part**.
That expense is gone: `self.k = {0,7,63,70}` already declares four 2x2 corner
blocks, so **the corner tap IS the selection.** No latch, no mode, nothing to
exit. Written into the entry header as the reading, not only into this SUMMARY.

**Why it matters, in the user's own terms, and it is the same complaint as the
suppression clause:** in a DAW, MIDI-learn binds whichever message arrives
first. With four CCs streaming from every touch, corner 3 cannot be bound to a
filter - the moment you hit learn, one of the other three lands first and takes
it. That is why *"mapping mode needed in"* sat in the same sentence as *"if
something doesn't change don't send it don't send 0 value"*: **both clauses are
about the pad shouting over itself.** 11-08 fixed the shouting; this makes each
corner individually reachable. **All three of MORPH's bench clauses are now
closed.**

### THE MEASUREMENT CAME FIRST, AND IT DID NOT SHRINK THE TASK

The plan predicted that 11-08's suppression might already deliver one message
from rest, in which case the real gap would be only a corner press arriving from
elsewhere. **Driven through the real Lua host before any change:**

| Probe | Messages before | Messages after |
|---|---|---|
| press on the exact extreme pixel `(0,0)` | **1** `(cc1,127)` | 1 |
| press on the **centre of the 2x2 corner block**, from rest | **4** `(cc1,100) (cc2,12) (cc3,12) (cc4,1)` | **1** `(cc1,100)` |
| the same, arriving after a stroke that left the others non-zero | **4**, identical values | **1** |

All four corners, before: **4 / 4 / 4 / 4** at the block centre from rest, and
**4 / 4 / 4 / 4** from elsewhere. After: **1 / 1 / 1 / 1** in every case, on a
slow tap and on a coalesced one alike.

**So the honest deliverable is NOT smaller than it looks, and the plan's
hypothesis is false at the point a hand actually reaches.** A finger aimed at a
corner lands in the BLOCK, not on the one pixel where the arithmetic is already
clean - at `(14,14)` the weights are 100/12/12/1 and all four leave. **The
prediction is only true on the single extreme pixel of each block.** Every probe
in the new test is therefore pinned to a point where all four weights are
non-zero, asserted before the tap, so a one-message result can never be the old
behaviour wearing the new one's clothes.

### The discrimination, costed twice

| Candidate | Verdict | The measurement |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) onset-in-corner emits one; any MOVE restores the four** | **CHOSEN** | works on both arrival shapes; the diagonal stroke is byte-identical |
| (b) only a coalesced DOWNUP (`e>8`) counts as a tap | **rejected, twice over** | see below |

**(b) is rejected by measurement, and the first half of the measurement is the
larger finding of the whole plan.** The shipped `src/lib/sim/touch.ts` **never
produces event code 9 at all** - it has three codes in it, 1, 4 and 5, and a
press held 300 ms and the fastest press a pointer can make **both deliver
`4@0(10,10) 5@0(10,10)`**. So option (b) would have been **invisible in the
browser entirely**, which is the same `PREV-01` failure that killed the
second-contact option on both entries. Second, on hardware a deliberate tap
aimed at a MIDI-learn button is exactly the slow kind that arrives as 4 then 5,
so the feature would be unreliable in the one situation it exists for.

### What ships, and what deliberately does not move

- The corner blocks are **derived from `self.k`** in the Lua (`s.k[j] + d%2 +
  d//2*9`, the same expression the paint loop already uses) and **read out of
  the entry's own source** in the test, never typed.
- **11-08's `s.p` is not bypassed, re-keyed or reset.** A corner tap updates
  `s.p` for the corner it spoke for and **leaves the other three alone on
  purpose**: they were not sent, so the receiver has not heard them, and the
  next continuous sample must be free to say so. **The honest price, stated
  rather than discovered:** tap corner 1 then corner 2 and a receiver holds
  corner 1 at its tapped value until a continuous stroke moves it. That is what
  "speaks for one corner" means, and it is what makes MIDI-learn work.
- **The paint stays unconditional.** A corner tap repaints all four corners'
  current weights even though it speaks for one - the picture is a readout and
  the wire is traffic.
- **The Timer is still the empty string and no keeper was added.** Counted
  inside the Lua bodies only, rather than by grepping the file: MORPH carries
  **1 `glt(` site** (the per-touch countdown) and **0 occurrences of 65535**.
  Three of the four `glt` hits a naive `grep -c` reports are comment prose,
  which is the "a comment can participate in a measurement" hazard this phase
  has hit twice already.
- **The cell index is bound once as `c` and used twice**, which gave 18
  characters back against the feature's cost.

### The captured diagonal-stroke literal

A stroke that does **not** begin in a corner - 64 samples down the diagonal from
the centre cell (64,64) into the bottom-right block - was captured against
`morph.ts` **as HEAD had it**, restored from a scratch copy, and committed into
`lua-smoke.spec.ts` as a literal of **160 `(cc, value)` pairs**:

```
16:31 17:31 18:31 19:32 16:30 19:33 16:29 19:34 16:28 19:35 16:27 19:36 ...
... 17:1 18:1 19:123 17:0 18:0 19:125 19:127
```

The post-change sequence is **byte-identical**, verified both by the spec and by
a direct `a === b` on the two captured JSON strings. It is also the clause that
proves a MOVE sample crossing a corner still morphs, because the stroke ends
inside corner block 4.

## Deviations from Plan

### 1. [Rule 1 - Bug] 11-09's amplitude probe was pressing the new stop button

- **Found during:** task 01, the first full run of `lua-smoke.spec.ts`
- **Issue:** *"moves ARC's heart and ARC's controller together, at three
  depths"* held `x = Math.round(0.5 * coordMax) = 64` at every depth, so at the
  middle depth it pressed `(64,64)` - **which is cell 40 exactly**. That is now
  the stop/resume toggle, so the mid-depth run tapped stop, the heart froze, the
  travel read 0 and the rank correlation went red.
- **Fix:** the probe's `x` moved to **40** (column 2, which no depth on that axis
  can turn into the centre cell). The property the test actually needs from `x`
  - that the oscillator rate is identical across all three runs - is unchanged:
  `1 + 40*31//127 = 10` at every depth.
- **Why this is a result and not a nuisance:** it is **the documented hole
  arriving as a measurement rather than as a claim**, and the collision is
  written down at the line where it happened rather than only here.
- **Files modified:** `src/lib/sim/lua-smoke.spec.ts`
- **Commit:** `907a1bd`

### 2. [Rule 2 - missing critical functionality] The keeper re-arm needed `or s.s<1`

- **Found during:** task 01, checking layer 2's lifetime under a stop
- **Issue:** ARC's keeper rides on `p<s.r`, true exactly on the phase wrap. A
  stopped card has no wrap, so **655 seconds after a stop the LED engine would
  expire layer 2 and the frozen swirl - the entire visible sign that the card is
  stopped - would go out.**
- **Fix:** `if p<s.r or s.s<1 then`, re-arming every tick while stopped. `+11`
  characters on a Timer with 646 free.
- **Commit:** `907a1bd`

### 3. [Design - `self.f`, which the plan did not name]

- The plan asked only that the resume restore the swirl. Restoring it by
  recomputing `glim(s.r//2,1,120)` would hand the card back at rate 2 when Setup
  armed it at 4, so **a stop-and-resume with no other gesture would visibly slow
  the swirl.** `self.f` carries the rate actually written. `+8` characters.

### 4. [Plan defect - the load-bearing sentence, named rather than absorbed]

- The plan's central claim is that `e==4 or e>=5` "stops and resumes inside one
  fast tap". **Measured: at ARC's site it does neither** - the naive spelling is
  behaviourally identical to the correct one because of 11-02's ended guard -
  **and where the trap IS live, it fires on the SLOW tap, not the fast one.**
  Recorded in the entry header and in the negative-check table above. **11-16
  owns the wording.**

## Everything the plan asserts that the tree does not support

1. **"A toggle written `e==4 or e>=5` fires on the down AND on the up of one
   fast tap."** Two errors in one sentence. It fires once at this site, because
   the ended guard already returns for `e>=5 and e<9`; and where a toggle *does*
   double-fire, the gesture that does it is the SLOW tap (4 then 5), not the
   fast one. Code 9 is the safe arrival. **Measured both ways, checks 1 and 1b.**
2. **"11-08's suppression already means three of the four corners are silent at
   a corner ... the honest deliverable may be smaller than it looks."** True only
   on the single extreme pixel of each block. **At the centre of a corner block,
   which is where a finger lands, the pre-change count is 4 from rest AND from
   elsewhere.** The deliverable is exactly as large as it looks.
3. **The plan's option (c) gate for ARC - "verify first whether `i>0` is
   reachable at all"** - is the same wrong gate 11-09 already reported. It **is**
   reachable. The option fails one step downstream, on a mouse having one
   pointer. Reported for the second consecutive wave.
4. **The plan's `must_haves.key_links` row for `arc.ts` gives the pattern as
   `e>8`.** The shipped text is `(e==4 or e>8)`, which contains it, so the row
   reads true - but it is worth noting that the parentheses are **required**:
   `and` binds tighter than `or` in Lua, so `e==4 or e>8 and c==40` would parse
   as `e==4 or (e>8 and c==40)` and the toggle would fire on every DOWN anywhere
   on the pad. A future author copying the bare pattern out of that row would
