gesture that both starts and ends in one message.

<interfaces>

## What SHUTTLE is today

A hold-to-scrub video jog. Press left of centre for reverse, right for forward; distance from centre
sets speed; **lift stops the transport**. A sixteen-cell ring shows the speed as an arc. Setup 663,
Timer 201.

**Read `shuttle.ts:80-92` before writing a line.** The lift-stops behaviour is not an accident and the
file says so. A redesign that reverses it owes both reasons an answer, and a redesign that keeps it
owes the user an explanation of why "rework" did not change it.

## The two shapes, and what each owes

### If the redesign keeps lift-stops

Then what changed is **legibility**, and the work is:

- **a centre marker** — a visitor cannot currently see where zero is;
- **direction colour** — forward and reverse must be distinguishable at a glance, not by memory;
- **a resting cue** — the pad should look like something that is waiting, not like something that is
  off;
- **card copy** that says *lift to stop* in the house register.

245 free is comfortable for that. **But D-04 says the verdict is on the behaviour**, so this shape
must argue that the behaviour was right and only unreadable — and if that argument cannot be made
honestly, it is the wrong shape. **Say which you concluded and why.**

### If the redesign latches

Then **both documented reasons must be re-solved**, and neither is trivial:

1. ***"Scrubs forever, stoppable only by touching the exact centre column."*** A latching transport
   needs a stop that is not a nine-pixel target. A dedicated stop cell, a whole stop row, a
   double-tap, or a lift-with-a-modifier are all candidates. **Cost at least two.**
2. ***A DOWNUP tap has no lift behind it.*** A fast tap that sets a speed must be stoppable by the
   same gesture that set it. Since code 9 both starts and ends the contact in one message, a
   latch-on-press design must decide what a *tap* means distinctly from what a *press-and-hold*
   means, and **the two must be distinguishable inside a single callback that may only ever see one
   event.** This is the harder half and it is where the design either works or does not. Work it
   through explicitly and **write the answer into the header**, because it is the exact trap that
   caught five entries in 11-02.

**A third shape is legitimate and should be considered rather than discovered**: a transport that
latches only above a speed threshold and springs back below it, or that latches on a hold and springs
on a tap. It answers both reasons structurally instead of by addition. **Cost it beside the other
two.**

## Constraints

**Motion.** SHUTTLE has a Timer, so it classifies as `animated` whatever its picture is doing — the
engine reports `host.animating || host.timerArmed`. If the redesign drops the Timer, **read
`frames.json`'s `animating` flag rather than reasoning about it**, and move `listing.ts` and any
front-door row with it.

**Copy.** *"Don't understand how it works"* is partly a copy failure whatever the behaviour becomes.
`listing.ts`'s description and the entry's `quiet` line both describe the current gesture. **Both move
with the redesign**, both are counted by script, and both are written in the house's plain register —
no metaphor, no exclamation, and the word "scrub" only if a visitor who has never edited video would
still understand the sentence.

**Tags.** One `FOR` and two `FEELS`, from the closed vocabulary of eight and six. `precise` and
`still` sit **exactly at the FEELS floor of 6** after 11-01, so **check the histogram before changing a
tag**, not after.

**The Timer floor.** Whatever the new period arithmetic is, it gets a `math.max(...,1)//1` floor.
`gtt(index, 0)` does not run fast — it stops, silently, with the picture still showing the last frame.
SHUTTLE carries the floor today **because this exact thing happened**; do not lose it in the rewrite.
