## The idiom, written once and cited twice

**The firmware event codes are 1 MOVE, 4 DOWN, 5 UP, and 9 DOWNUP** (`pad-sim.ts:234-241`, and
`zona-docs/docs/ZONA_REFERENCE.md` s4.6). They are **CITED, never restated** in an entry header.

**The discrete-tap edge is `e==4 or e>8`, and the continuous stream is `e==1`.**

| Code | What it is | Fires the toggle? | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | DOWN | **yes** | the onset of a press |
| 9 | DOWNUP | **yes** | a sub-cycle tap coalesced into ONE message - a whole press, and there is no UP behind it |
| 1 | MOVE | no | the finger is travelling; this is the continuous gesture |
| 5 | UP | **no** | the release of a press whose DOWN already fired |
| 3 | ENDED | no | contact gone |

**This is the load-bearing sentence of the plan.** A toggle written as `e==4 or e>=5` fires on the down
AND on the up of one slow press: it stops and resumes inside one gesture and **looks exactly like a
card that does nothing.** 11-02 admitted code 9 as a real press at six guards precisely so that a fast
tap is not silently dropped, and the same admission is what makes the naive `e>=5` form live rather
than theoretical here.

**STAGE already ships this exact discrimination** - `if e==4 or e>8 then … elseif e>=5 then` - and it
is the one `DECLARED_EXCEPTIONS` row in `touch-guard.spec.ts`. **Both headers written here cite
`stage.ts` for the form rather than re-deriving it**, and neither entry earns a new exception row: an
`e==4 or e>8` onset test with no unbounded `elseif` behind it is what the gate wants, not what it
forbids. **Re-run `touch-guard.spec.ts` and confirm `DECLARED_EXCEPTIONS.length` is still 2.**

### Where the state lives

```lua
-- Setup, today
self.r=4 self.d=127 self.h=0
self.touch_cb=function(s,i,e,x,y)
  if i>0 or e==3 or e>=5 and e<9 then return end
  s.d=127-y
  local r=1+x*31//127
  if r~=s.r then s.r=r local f=glim(r//2,1,120)for a=0,80 do glf(a,2,f)end end
end

-- Timer, today
local p=(s.h+s.r)%256 s.h=p
```

`s.r` is the rate and `s.h` is the phase. **Stopping must not be done by zeroing `s.r`**: the touch
handler recomputes `r = 1 + x*31//127` on every accepted sample and `r` is at least 1 at every x, so a
zeroed rate is revived by the next touch - including the very touch that is meant to resume it, which
would make the toggle look like it works in one direction only. **Carry a separate flag.**

### The centre cell, and the two gestures to cost

**Cost at least two and state the one chosen and why**, in the entry header, in the SUMMARY's own
words.

- **(a) The centre CELL, index 40.** ARC's heart is painted at `40+j*9+k` for `j,k` in -1..1 - the
  centre 3x3 block - so cell 40 is the middle pixel of a shape the card already draws. A press there is
  aimed at something visible. Cost: one cell test on the onset edge. **The hole it leaves**: cell 40 is
  the exact middle of the x and y mapping, so the rate and depth values that cell would have set become
  unreachable by a press *starting* there. Measure what those values are and state them - a hole at
  `r = 16, d = 63` is a different sentence from a hole at an end of the range.
- **(b) The centre 3x3 BLOCK, the whole heart.** A bigger target, which is the more forgiving thing on
  a pad with no labels. **The hole it leaves is nine times bigger**, and the heart is exactly where a
  user aiming for "medium rate, medium depth" would press.
- **(c) A second contact.** ARC's guard returns for `i>0`, so a second finger is currently ignored
  entirely and is free to mean something. **Verify first whether `i>0` is reachable at all through the
  Lua host and the shipped `TouchSampler`** - if the simulator can never deliver it, the preview cannot
  show the feature and it must not be chosen, because a card whose headline gesture is invisible in the
  browser is the failure `PREV-01` exists to prevent. If it is unreachable, say so with the measurement
  and pick from (a) or (b).

**Whichever is chosen, the SAMPLE THAT TOGGLES MUST NOT ALSO SET THE RATE AND DEPTH**, or the gesture
that stops the card also jerks its rate. Return after toggling.

### The stopped picture, and it must be costed twice as well

A user has to be able to see that it is stopped. **Two candidates, both cheap, and the difference is
what a stopped ARC looks like across a room:**

- **(i) The heart alone freezes.** `v` stops advancing, so the 3x3 heart holds a constant brightness.
  Free - it falls out of freezing the phase. **The risk, and it is real: if the phase happens to be
  near 0 when it stops, the heart freezes BLACK and a stopped card is indistinguishable from a dead
  one.** Measure where the heart actually lands across a spread of stop moments and report the range;
  if black is reachable, this candidate needs a floor and the floor costs characters.
- **(ii) The heart freezes and the swirl stops with it.** `for a=0,80 do glf(a,2,0) end` on stop and
  the existing `glim(s.r//2,1,120)` on resume - the same 81-write shape the rate-change branch already
  performs, so it is an established cost rather than a new one. A stopped ARC is then unmistakably
  still. **`glf(...,0)` beside the existing `glt(a,2,65535)` is a zero rate beside a keeper, which is
  the LEGITIMATE form** - it is a static layer, not a decay - but `decay-idiom.spec.ts` must be re-run
  and its verdict stated rather than predicted.

State both costs in characters against ARC's 518 free Setup, pick one, and write the rejected one into
the header with the reason. **Do not ship both.**

### Repeat traffic while stopped

While stopped, the Timer still runs at 20 ms and `s:gms(...)` still fires with an unchanging value -
**which is the same shouting 11-08 fixed for MORPH.** Whether to suppress it is a real decision and
**both answers are defensible**: suppression is quieter and matches the phase's own precedent, and
non-suppression means a host that connects mid-stop still learns the value. **Measure the message
count over a stopped second (50 samples at 20 ms), state it, choose, and write the reason in the
header.** If suppression ships, it is `s.p`-shaped like MORPH's and it is cited to `morph.ts`, not
re-derived. **If it does not ship, say the number out loud** rather than leaving it to be found.

## MORPH - one tap, one message

**Re-measured at the picker corner as 11-08 left it: Setup 579 of 908, 329 free; Timer 0.** MORPH's
header is already correct about the corner and says why (`@TRAILC` declares `255,255,255`), and it

### The shape

```lua
self.k={0,7,63,70}   -- four 2x2 corner blocks: cells k+d%2+d//2*9 for d=0..3
self.p={0,0,0,0}     -- 11-08's per-corner last-sent value
-- corner j owns CC @CCB+j and 2x2 cells at self.k[j+1]
```

The cell under a finger is `x*9//128+y*9//128*9`. **The four corner blocks are cells
{0,1,9,10}, {7,8,16,17}, {63,64,72,73}, {70,71,79,80}** - derive them in the test rather than typing
them, so a change to `self.k` reddens the test instead of silently escaping it.

### The one design question

**A corner tap must be distinguishable from the start of a continuous morph that begins in a corner.**
Cost at least two, choose one, and state why in the header.

- **(a) Onset-in-corner emits one, MOVE restores the four.** On the onset edge (`e==4 or e>8`), if the
  cell is inside a corner block, emit that corner's CC only and return. On any MOVE sample, the
  existing bilinear branch runs unchanged. So: touch a corner and hold - one message, which is what
  MIDI-learn needs; touch a corner and drag - one message, then the full morph from 10 ms later.
  **The observation that makes this nearly free: 11-08's suppression already means three of the four
  corners are silent at a corner anyway**, because at a corner three weights are exactly 0 and `s.p`
  holds them at 0 unless the finger arrived from elsewhere. **Measure how many messages a corner press
  emits TODAY, before the change** - if the answer is already 1 in the common case, the honest
  deliverable is smaller than it looks and the SUMMARY must say so rather than claim a fix for traffic
  that was not there. **This measurement decides whether the feature is worth its characters and it
  comes FIRST.**
- **(b) Only a coalesced DOWNUP (`e>8`) counts as a tap.** Cheaper still - one comparison - but it ties
  the feature to how fast the user's finger is, and a deliberate, careful tap aimed at a MIDI-learn
  button is exactly the slow kind. **Measure what a deliberate tap actually delivers through the
  shipped `TouchSampler`** before rejecting or choosing this; if a slow tap arrives as 4 then 5, this
  option makes the feature unreliable in the one situation it exists for, and that sentence needs a
  measurement behind it rather than an intuition.

### What must NOT change

**The continuous bilinear morph is unchanged**, and that is asserted rather than promised: capture the
full ordered `(cc, value)` sequence for a stroke that does **not** begin in a corner - a diagonal
through the middle - **before** the change, commit it as a literal, and assert it byte-identical
after. This is the non-vacuity guard, and it is the same discipline 11-09's task 01 used to pin
POMODORO's four indices with four captured stamps.

**11-08's per-corner suppression stays.** `s.p` is not reset, re-keyed or bypassed by the new branch;
if a corner tap emits CC *j*, `s.p[j]` is updated exactly as the continuous path updates it, or the
next continuous sample will re-send a value the receiver already has.

**The paint stays unconditional**, per MORPH's header: the picture is a readout and the wire is
traffic. A corner tap paints all four corners' current weights even though it speaks for one.
