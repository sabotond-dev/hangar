## The comet decay: seven hunks, +0 characters

**The arithmetic.** For a decay, `step = 256 - rate` and the freeze is
`(start + rate x timeout) mod 256`, which equals `(start - step x timeout) mod 256`. To land on 0 the
start must equal `step x timeout`. `DECAY_TABLE`'s twelve rows:

| rate | step | ticks | `step x ticks` | start today | freeze today |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 244 | 12 | 21 | **252** | 255 | 3 |
| 245 | 11 | 23 | **253** | 255 | 2 |
| 246 | 10 | 25 | **250** | 255 | 5 |
| 247 | 9 | 28 | **252** | 255 | 3 |
| 248 | 8 | 31 | **248** | 255 | 7 |
| 249 | 7 | 36 | **252** | 255 | 3 |
| 250 | 6 | 42 | **252** | 255 | 3 |
| 251 | 5 | 50 | **250** | 255 | 5 |
| 252 | 4 | 63 | **252** | 255 | 3 |
| 253 | 3 | 84 | **252** | 255 | 3 |
| 254 | 2 | 127 | **254** | 255 | 1 |
| 255 | 1 | 254 | **254** | 255 | 1 |

**The fix is one expression: emit `(256 - rate) * ticks` as the start instead of the literal 255.**
Every value is three digits, so the emitted Lua is the **same length** — `+0` characters at every
site, at every knob position. Verify that claim per site rather than trusting this table.

`DECAY_TABLE` itself is **not** edited: no set of ticks makes `step x ticks` equal 255 for every row
(255 = 3 x 5 x 17 admits only steps 1, 3, 5, 15, 17, 51, 85, 255, and the table's steps are 1..12), so
the start has to move rather than the table.

## The compiled fast tap: measure first, then change what is wrong and affordable

`ENDED` and `LIVE` are interpolated at `_pad.ts:1220, 1256, 1671, 1688, 2000, 2069, 2076, 2123, 2132,
2241`. The correct forms are `e==3 or e>=5 and e<9` (**+8**) and, for LIVE,
`e~=3 and e<5 or e>8` — Lua binds `and` tighter than `or`, so that parses as
`(e~=3 and e<5) or (e>8)`, which is what is wanted, and it is **+7**.

**`tpad` is excluded, by name, for two independent reasons — either would be sufficient:**

1. **It already handles code 9 correctly, by structure.** `:2241` reads
   `local c,t=s.p[i],${ENDED}` and the next line is `if e==4 or e>7 or not c and not t then`, which
   routes 9 through the **onset** branch, and `if t then` closes the contact in the same pass. Making
   `ENDED` false for 9 would start a trackpad contact **that never ends** — a stuck pointer.
2. **It cannot afford it.** tpad's compiled Setup is **902 of 908**, six characters free, 907 at the
   worst reachable knob position. `+8` puts it over budget and the sweep's 908 gate goes red.

So the fix is per-site, not per-constant. Introduce a second named constant rather than editing
`ENDED` in place, so the trackpad site keeps the old text and the change is visible as an addition:
the manifest row then says *"a new constant, applied at N of eleven sites; `:2241` keeps the original
because it routes code 9 through its own onset branch and has six characters free."*

**Measure before deciding.** For each of the nine presets, drive `touchTap` on a representative cell
and a slow down/move/up on the same cell through the real Lua host and count the MIDI both ways. The
research did this for the twenty-seven Lua entries and for none of these. **Present the table before
changing anything**, and change only the sites where the fast tap demonstrably misbehaves **and** the
preset can afford the characters at its worst knob position.

**Any site that is wrong and unaffordable is a finding**: report the shortfall in characters and the
behaviour that stays broken, and let 11-16 record it. Do not trim a preset to make room.

## The proof the boundary held

`src/lib/fidelity/firmware-oracle.spec.ts` compares the simulator against constants re-derived from
`grid-fw` by an author who never read the simulator. **It is not edited by this plan.** Green after
these hunks is the mechanical evidence that `pha += fre` was not touched — a start-constant edit
leaves it green, a phase-walk edit turns it red. **State its green run as the evidence, and prove the
claim with a negative check** rather than asserting it.

**Why that asymmetry is real rather than lucky, which neither this plan nor 11-03 previously said.**
The oracle's **seventh** test, *"agrees on the tick order at layer expiry"* (`:213`), builds
`defaultState()`, sets `enabled.look = false` and `enabled.sends = false`, leaves
`enabled.touch = true` and drives one `touchDown`. `DEFAULT_PAD_STATE.touch.kind` is **`"comet"`**
(`_pad.ts:637`), and disabling Look removes the keeper that would otherwise re-arm forever — **so the
only layer ticking in that test is the comet layer painted at `pad-sim.ts:986-988`, and `:987` is
the `glpfs(h, 1, 255, decay.rate, 0)` line in row 4 of the table above.** The test does not sit next
to this plan's edit; it runs straight through it.

**And every one of its assertions is relative to an observed expiry rather than to an absolute tick.**
It scans for the `animating` true-to-false transition, names that tick `expiry`, and asserts
`hashes[expiry + 1] === hashes[expiry - 1 + offset]` with `offset` read from the oracle's
`TICK_ORDER.phaseAdvanceOffsetAtExpiry`. **Change the start constant and `expiry` moves; every index
moves with it and the test stays green.** `(256 - rate) * ticks` is by construction the start from
which the walk reaches 0 in exactly `ticks` steps, so it moves *when* the layer expires and not *which
frame relative to expiry* is frozen. **Clamp the phase walk and the frozen frame changes position
relative to `expiry`, and the test goes red.** That is the whole proof, and it is why the negative
check below plants its change on `& 255` and on nothing else. **Write this reasoning into the SUMMARY**
— "a standing gate stayed green" is a weaker claim than the one this phase is actually entitled to.
