<objective>
> STRIP: *"this is just an XY pad, it should be two faders, one crossfader at the bottom and the big
> one, sending midi independently"*

The ask is precise and it is a redesign rather than a fix. **What matters is naming what is being
given up**, because STRIP today is not a plain XY pad and the thing it actually is happens to be the
repository's only worked example of two techniques.

**STRIP is a 14-bit fader.** The bottom row is a **vernier** and the two axes combine into one
`glim(v*8+f,0,16383)` on mode 1. It opens with `self:txma(1023)self:tyma(1023)` — **the repository's
only use of the 10-bit coordinate unlock** — and it is the only entry that needs it, because
coordinate resolution matters for continuous output and for nothing else.

**Two independent faders plus a crossfader is three streams and almost certainly 7-bit each.** That is
a deliberate trade, not an oversight, and it must be named in the plan and in the entry's header:
the card is exchanging **one very precise control for three ordinary ones**, which is exactly what the
user asked for.

Budget: **638 Setup of 908, 270 free. No Timer at all**, so a full 908 is available if the design
wants one — though a card of three static faders probably should not have one, since **any entry with
a stored Timer classifies as `animated`** whatever its picture is doing.

Purpose: the ask, delivered, with the trade on the record.
Output: one re-authored entry, two behaviour tests, and a named loss.

## What STRIP is today, and what it costs to give up

`strip.ts:127`, Setup only, 638 of 908:

```lua
self:txma(1023) self:tyma(1023)
...
local v=(1023-y)*2047//1023
local f=x*8//1024
s:gms(@CH,176,@CC,glim(v*8+f,0,16383),1)
local h=v*9//2048
```

Three things go with the redesign, and **each is named rather than mourned**:

1. **The 14-bit stream.** One controller carrying 0..16383. Three 7-bit streams is what the user
   asked for and it is the right answer to their note; it is still a loss and the header says so.
2. **The vernier.** The bottom row is a fine-adjust for the main axis — an elegant use of a row that
   most cards waste. **The crossfader now wants that row**, which is why the two designs cannot
   coexist.
3. **The 10-bit unlock.** `self:txma(1023)self:tyma(1023)` costs **+30** and is the repository's only
   worked example.

**Decide deliberately whether the unlock survives, and the answer is probably yes for one of the three
controls.** The relevant arithmetic, which is why no cell-grid card should ever spend it: firmware
reports 0..127 by dividing the native 0..1023 by 8, so for a nine-cell grid each column is 14.22 units
wide and 7-bit quantisation locates a column boundary to within about **0.07 of a cell**. Unlocking
10 bits improves that to 0.009 of a cell — imperceptible. **It matters only for continuous output.**
A crossfader read as a continuous position is continuous output; three faders quantised to nine rows
are not. **State which controls, if any, keep it, and why**, with that arithmetic as the reason rather
than a preference.

**If the unlock is dropped entirely, say so and say what it costs the repository**: the only worked
example of `txma`/`tyma` disappears, and the next card that needs it starts from the firmware docs.
That is worth one sentence in the header — the same rescue reasoning 11-01 applied to `life.ts` and
`gridlock.ts`, applied to a technique rather than to an idiom.

## The layout, which is the design problem

Nine columns and nine rows for **two vertical faders plus one horizontal crossfader**. The obvious
reading — *"one crossfader at the bottom and the big one"* — suggests the crossfader owns the bottom
row (or two) and the faders share what is left. That still leaves choices worth costing:

- **How wide is each fader?** Two columns each with a gap, four each, or the full remaining width
  split down the middle. A wider target is easier to hit; a gap makes them readable as two things.
- **How many rows does the crossfader take?** One row is 9 steps and is thin under a finger. Two rows
  is a fatter target and costs the faders a step.
- **Which is "the big one"?** The user names one fader as big, which reads as **asymmetric**: one wide
  fader and one narrow, not two equal ones. **That is a real reading of their words and it should not
  be flattened into symmetry for tidiness.** If symmetry is chosen instead, say why.

**Each control must be distinguishable on the pad.** `console.ts` is the shipped precedent for
multi-fader colour and rail treatment on this hardware — read it rather than inventing a scheme, and
say what was borrowed.

**Three independent streams means three controller numbers.** `@CC`, `@CC+1`, `@CC+2` is the pattern
`console.ts` and `lumen.ts` already use. If a knob is added, it is **appended**.

## What the tests must prove

*"Sending midi independently"* is the whole ask, and it is testable exactly:

- moving one control emits on its controller and **on neither of the other two** — this is the
  assertion that fails if the axes are still combined anywhere;
- each control spans its full 0..127, top to bottom edge, with **127 reachable** — the same defect
  CONSOLE had at 111, and the same kind of check;
- the 14-bit loss is recorded as a test rather than as a comment: assert the emitted value fits 7 bits
  on each stream, so a future re-introduction of the vernier is a deliberate change to a test rather
  than a silent one.
