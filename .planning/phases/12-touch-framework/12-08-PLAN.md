<objective>
The three sequencers onto the library - the entries the probe was run for.

> `EUCLID: still not precise.` · `STEPS: same as the other sequencers` · `RADAR POINTS: nice but needs
> the touch detection framework.`

**What "not precise" was, settled by Q2.** `71*9//128 = 4` and `72*9//128 = 5`: the boundary between
two cells is one unit wide, and a still finger wobbles one unit every sample. A finger anywhere near an
edge is read as alternating taps on two cells - the per-contact dedup 11-08 gave these three toggles
twice. Phase 11 read the same complaint as fast-tap loss and fixed code 9 everywhere; Q3 shows the
hardware never produces a 9 for a human tap, so those fixes were correct and harmless and not the
answer. **The answer is hysteresis, and the library carries it.**

**Each re-fit replaces an inlined guard with one call.** All three carry, verbatim,
`if e~=1 and e~=4 and e<9 then s.q[i]=nil return end local m=x*9//128+y*9//128*9 if s.q[i]==m then return end s.q[i]=e<9 and m`
plus `self.q={}` in Setup. The replacement is `local m=Q(s,i,e,x,y)if not m then return end` and
the table declaration goes. The research measured the saving at 92 / 87 / 92 with the four-argument
call; the five-argument call costs two more each. **Re-measure; do not copy.**

**What the sequencers do not take from the library, and why.** Not `F` - their armed cells toggle
under the finger, which is already the feedback. Not `X` - they hold no per-contact note, and a stale
`H[i]` is cleared by the next press on that cell through `Q`'s own rule; a Timer sweep would cost
budget for nothing. Not `R` - nothing to release. Say so in each header so the next reader does not
add them.

Purpose: three bench lines closed by the mechanism the probe found, at a saving.
Output: three entries re-fitted, one test, no picture moved.
