## What shipped

### Task 1 — the keyboard, the round trip and the hazard (commit `5908afc`)

Three tests appended to `e2e/browse.e2e.ts`, taking it from 8 to **11** chromium tests.

**Test 9, "the keyboard crosses the grid in one tab stop and opens a configuration."** Cold
`/browse/`, focus the search field, then `Tab` until focus is inside the grid — **counted, not
written down**, because how many stops sit between the field and the grid is derived from the tag
data (W-19: the standing row is every tag on two or more configurations). Observed: **11 Tab
presses**, and the loop is bounded at 60 so an unreachable grid goes red instead of hanging.

Then the roving tabindex, which is the whole of W-07: exactly **one** `[tabindex="0"]` inside the
grid and **fifteen** at `-1`, focus on the first card's anchor. The one-tab-stop claim is asserted by
**leaving**: one more `Tab` and focus is outside the grid entirely, then `Shift+Tab` comes back to
the same roving card.

The column count is read off the live layout and parsed by the **shipped** parser:

```ts
const columns = columnsFromTemplate(template);
```

where `template` is `getComputedStyle(grid).gridTemplateColumns` read in the page. `grid.spec.ts`
pins `columnsFromTemplate` in node; this is the browser half of the same arithmetic. A hard-coded 4
would pass at 1280x720 and mislead at every other width. `ArrowRight` twice lands on cards 1 and 2,
`ArrowDown` lands on card `2 + columns`, `End` on the last card, and `ArrowDown` from there **does
not move** — the ends are the ends. `Enter` follows the anchor natively (no handler) onto
`/c/tpad/`.

**Test 10, "browse, open a configuration, and come back to the same view."** `NEWEST`, the query
`pad`, the `playable` chip — three cards, computed by the shipped `filterListing` and `sortListing`
rather than transcribed. The address is waited for (it is projected on a 500 ms trailing timer) and
recorded whole. The page is scrolled to the bottom and the offset recorded as a number, with the
greater-than-zero check as its precondition: restoring an offset of 0 would prove nothing.

Observed, printed by the test:

```
browse round trip: scrollY 573 before, 573 after (tolerance 2px)
```

**The tolerance is 2 px and it is stated in the test**, as `SCROLL_TOLERANCE_PX`. The measured slack
was zero.

Coming back is the header slot reading `BACK TO BROWSE`; after it the address, the three cards in
order, the checked `NEWEST` radio, the field's value and the checked chip are all asserted. Then one
`goBack()` — which lands on `/c/ninepads/`, **not** on a second copy of the browse screen — and the
`browse` test id is asserted absent. Finally, a page opened with `context.newPage()` (a fresh tab, so
a fresh `sessionStorage`, asserted empty as a precondition) cold-loads `/c/euclid/` and reads
`BROWSE ALL`.

> **A correction to the plan's own gloss, recorded rather than quietly absorbed.** The plan describes
> that Back press as showing "the round trip did not grow the history". It did grow it, by exactly
> one entry, and that is the shipped decision: `BrowseLink.svelte` ships `goto(href, { noScroll:
> true })` and deliberately **not** `{ replaceState: true }`, because 05.1-09 measured `replaceState`
> taking Kit's shallow popstate branch on this exact journey and leaving the address bar reading
> `/c/ghost/` over a browse screen that was still on the page. Deferred item 2 already records that
> `05.1-UI-SPEC.md`'s sentence asking for `replaceState` is the thing that is wrong. The assertion
> the plan asks for — one Back press leaves browse — is true and is made; only its explanation moves.

**Test 11, "un-choosing a hand-authored configuration leaves its pad running."** Cold `/c/euclid/`,
where the row holds EUCLID **alone** (asserted: exactly one `pad-canvas-*` on the page). The
precondition is asserted first — two samples 400 ms apart differ, so the pad was really running
before anything was chosen. A quick tap chooses it; the panel and the tuning region appear; **both
meters are then waited on for `aria-busy="false"`**, which is the part that arms the test: ownership
of the engine transfers at `onpreview`, and an `Escape` pressed before the handover exercises the
branch where `destroy()` closes an engine nobody ever saw — the safe case, not the hazard. Then
`Escape`, and two more samples 400 ms apart, asserted to differ.

## The WebKit still-pad result, with its numbers

The question the test is near — **does a canvas keep its bitmap when its `<li>` is moved by a keyed
`{#each}`** — is genuinely unspecified across engines, and this repository deliberately does not
depend on the answer: `BrowseGrid.svelte` awaits Svelte's `tick()` after any reorder and calls
`SimHost.repaintAll()`, which repaints every registered pad from its engine's current frame whether
or not it is on screen. That is what makes the outcome **deterministic and therefore assertable**
rather than a hope. The counts are recorded anyway, because the underlying question is worth having
evidence about either way.

Observed, identically on **both** projects, over a `FEATURED` → `NAME` sort:

```
ninepads lit cells across a sort: 81 before at index 6, 81 after at index 10 (of 81)
```

Nine pads lights all eighty-one cells; it is declared `static` and not `restsBlack`; and it really
moved in the DOM, from index 6 to index 10, which the test asserts before it looks at the canvas.

> **Why the entry is named rather than fully derived.** The first draft derived it —
> `LISTING.filter(e => e.motion === "static" && !e.restsBlack)` — and got **Joystick**, whose still
> frame is a **single lit cell of 81**. The assertion was true and rested on one pixel. The shipped
> version names `ninepads` and `faders` in preference order (the two the plan names) and then checks
> their two declared facts against the catalog, so the declaration is still gated by data and the
> evidence is a picture rather than a dot. Both counts are printed either way.

```ts
// perturbation
```

**RED, observed:**

## The two requirements, closed with their qualifiers

### CAT-02 — sort by Featured, Newest and Name; no popularity metric

**Closed.** The qualifier is about **provenance, not behaviour**, and the direction of it matters:

- The three orders **are** the shipped comparators in `src/lib/catalog/index.ts` as `05.1-CONTEXT.md`'s
  **amended** D-10 has them: `byFeatured()` (featured first, then name, with no `addedAt` tie-break),
  `byNewest()`, and a plain code-point `nameAsc`. No `localeCompare`, no `Intl.Collator`, anywhere.
- **`05.1-UI-SPEC.md` W-08 AGREES with this and is NOT superseded.** The spec approved on 2026-09-04
  states the same rule in W-08 and names `byFeatured()`, `byNewest()` and `nameAsc` in its sort
  table. What the amendment supersedes is **`05.1-RESEARCH.md`** — its Standard Stack row and its
  §Don't Hand-Roll row, both recommending `Intl.Collator("en", { sensitivity: "base" })`. Saying it
  the other way round misattributes the divergence and invites somebody to "fix" the approved spec.
- `nameAsc` is **not exported** from `src/lib/catalog/index.ts`, so `src/lib/browse/sort.ts` restates
  it rather than importing it. The restatement is gated: `sort.spec.ts` tests 2, 3 and 4 hold it
  against `byFeatured()`, `byNewest()` and `byName()` id sequences, and test 5 asserts mechanically
  that neither `localeCompare` nor `Intl` is named.
- The **no popularity metric** half is held by `browse-ui.spec.ts` test 1 as a comment-stripped
  source scan with fragment-assembled needles — not by inspection — and by W-04 refusing a count on
  any chip.

### 2. [Rule 1 — Correction] The plan's gloss on the Back press is wrong; the assertion is not

**Found during:** Task 1, writing test 10.

opposite of what the plan asks to assert.
**Fix:** The assertion the plan wants is made and passes. Its **reason** is written into the test as
a comment citing 05.1-09's measurement and deferred item 2, so nobody later "fixes" the control to
match the gloss and breaks the browser's Back button again.
**Files:** `e2e/browse.e2e.ts`. **Commit:** `5908afc`.
