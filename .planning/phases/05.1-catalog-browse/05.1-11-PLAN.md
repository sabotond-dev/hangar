the production build.

**The Lua un-choose test is the one that matters most**, because it is the browser half of a hazard
this phase created and then closed. `buildTuner`'s `destroy()` used to close the engine the coverflow
was also painting from. That was harmless while every Lua entry stayed out of `FRONT_DOOR` — and plan
05.1-05 made `/c/euclid/` real, where the row is EUCLID **alone**, so un-choosing would have closed the
VM behind the only pad on the page and blanked it. Plan 05.1-04 fixed it and pinned it in node; this
proves it where a visitor would have seen it.

**The e2e arithmetic, so the totals are not a guess.** The `webkit-phone` project carries
`grep: /@webkit/` and the chromium project carries no grep, so **a title tagged `@webkit` runs in both
projects and costs 2**; an untagged one costs 1. This plan adds three untagged titles to
`e2e/browse.e2e.ts` and three tagged titles in a new file: `3 + (3 x 2) = 9`.

**And two claims are recorded rather than gated**, deliberately. The sixteen-canvas frame budget was
measured in plan 05.1-10 with no threshold. The WebKit still-pad-after-sort test **does** assert, and
this plan says why: `SimHost.repaintAll()` makes it deterministic rather than a hope about canvas
bitmap survival, so it is a real contract; the measured lit-pixel counts before and after are recorded
alongside so the number is on the file either way.

<interfaces>
**The three new chromium tests** (`e2e/browse.e2e.ts` goes from 7 to 10):

8. **"the keyboard crosses the grid in one tab stop and opens a configuration"** — cold `/browse/`,
   `Tab` from the search field through the toolbar into the grid, assert exactly **one** element inside
   `browse-grid` has `tabindex="0"`, press `ArrowRight` twice and `ArrowDown` once and assert
   `document.activeElement` is the expected card's anchor each time (compute the expected index with
   the live column count read from the grid, not from a hard-coded number), press `End` and assert the
   last card, press `ArrowDown` again and assert it **did not move** (the ends are the ends), then
   press `Enter` and assert the browser is on that configuration's `/c/<id>/`.
9. **"browse, open a configuration, and come back to the same view"** — cold `/browse/`, set
   `NEWEST`, type a query, press a chip, scroll to the bottom of the grid, record `window.scrollY`,
   open a card, assert the header slot reads `BACK TO BROWSE`, press it, and assert: the address, the
   selected sort, the query, the active chips and `window.scrollY` all match what was recorded (allow
   a small tolerance on the offset and state it). Then assert one Back press from there leaves browse
   entirely — the round trip did not grow the history. Finally, in a fresh context, cold-load
   `/c/euclid/` and assert the slot reads `BROWSE ALL`.
10. **"un-choosing a hand-authored configuration leaves its pad running"** — cold `/c/euclid/`; wait
    for the pad to have a picture; tap the pad to choose; wait for the tuning region; press `Escape`
    to un-choose; then sample `pad-canvas-euclid` twice, 400 ms apart, and assert the two samples
    **differ** — the pad is still being painted by a live engine. Assert the console has no error, and
    assert the precondition (that the pad was animating before the choose) so the test cannot pass on a
    pad that was never running.

**The three `@webkit` tests** (`e2e/browse-webkit.e2e.ts`, new file, every title tagged):

1. **"the browse screen renders on a phone @webkit"** — cold `/browse/` at the iPhone 15 viewport;
   assert sixteen cards, the count line, one column (the UI spec's table says one below 544px content
   width), and no horizontal overflow (`document.documentElement.scrollWidth <=
   document.documentElement.clientWidth`).
2. **"a still configuration stays lit after a sort @webkit"** — filter or scroll to a **still, lit**
   configuration (`ninepads` or `faders`, both declared `static` and neither `restsBlack`); sample its
   canvas; change the sort so its card moves in the DOM; sample again. Assert the pad is **still lit**
   — count non-black pixels before and after and assert both are above zero — and **record both
   counts**. This asserts rather than merely records because `SimHost.repaintAll()` makes the outcome
   deterministic; the numbers are recorded because the underlying question (does a canvas bitmap
   survive a re-parenting move on WebKit) is genuinely unverified and the evidence is worth keeping.
3. **"the search field does not zoom the viewport @webkit"** — assert the field's computed
   `font-size` is `16px` and that focusing it does not change `window.visualViewport.scale` (or, if
   that is unavailable, that the layout viewport width is unchanged). iOS Safari zooms for a field
   below 16px and never zooms back, which is why the number is in the spec rather than a preference.
