### The `aria-disabled` negative check, observed and restored

The real `disabled` attribute was replaced by `aria-disabled={disabled ? "true" : undefined}` and the
same page driven again:

| Attribute                | What the browser did with a click on `gestural`                                             |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| real `disabled`          | toggles stayed **2**, the chip stayed unchecked, the grid stayed at **2 configurations**      |
| `aria-disabled="true"`   | toggles **2 → 3**, `checked` became **true**, the grid went **2 → 0 configurations**          |

Playwright corroborates the distinction from a second direction: it refuses to click **either** form
without `force`, reporting `element is not enabled` for both — so the ARIA attribute does communicate
"unavailable" to a tool that reads the tree. It just does not **prevent** anything. A forced click on
the real attribute changes nothing; a forced click on the ARIA one empties the grid, which is precisely
the "I pressed it and the page broke" the spec's insistence on the real attribute exists to stop.

**Restored** to the real attribute, and the restored file re-observed: `first disabled chip:
tag-gestural`, `toggles before 2, after clicking tag-gestural: 2`.
